Most music festivals ask you to travel to a field. Ziro asks you to cross a state border with a permit, drive four hours up a hill road, and then sleep beside rice paddies that the Apatani people have farmed for generations. That is exactly why people go back. We have sent guests to Ziro every September for years, and the ones who enjoy it most are the ones who plan it as a trip to a valley, not just a gig.

The festival started in 2012, founded by Bobby Hano and Anup Kutty, and is run with members of the Apatani community. It is four days of mostly independent and folk-rooted music, with a strong line-up from the Northeast alongside Indian and international acts. The 2026 edition, the 13th, ran from 24 to 27 September with more than 40 artists.

## Dates and how the festival works

The festival usually falls in the last week of September, and the organisers announce dates and the first artists several months before. Passes are sold on the official ticketing site, often in tiers that rise as the festival approaches. We do not quote a ticket price here because it changes every edition; check the official site the day you plan to book.

A typical day starts slowly. Afternoon sets begin after lunch, and the main stage runs well into the night. In between, there is local food, Apatani rice beer and a great deal of standing around in the mud with strangers who become friends. The 2026 edition added a jam space and craft workshops rooted in Apatani traditions, and each year brings some new experiment alongside the music.

Pay attention to the last shuttle times. The festival ground is outside the main town, and once the last shuttle leaves, finding a vehicle back to Hapoli at 1 am is not straightforward.

## Permits: e-ILP and PAP

Ziro is in Arunachal Pradesh, so every Indian visitor needs an Inner Line Permit, and the state issues it online as an e-ILP. Under guidelines notified in March 2026, tourists get a Temporary ILP valid for 15 days across the state, approved online after Aadhaar OTP verification. That comfortably covers a four-day festival plus travel days. Official pages still show different fees, so budget about ₹300–₹500 per person and pay whatever the portal shows. Carry the ILP and your original ID; the Banderdewa and Kimin gates are the usual checkpoints on the Ziro side. Our [Arunachal ILP guide](/guides/arunachal-ilp-guide/) covers the photo size, document upload and checkpoints step by step.

Foreign nationals need a Protected Area Permit. It is filed through an approved Arunachal tour operator, typically takes two weeks or more, and carries a state royalty of about USD 30–50 per person, which your operator will confirm. Solo foreign travellers are reported to be allowed for Ziro, unlike many other circuits. Start that process as soon as you have booked your pass. Check current status before you travel, because both the fee and processing rules have changed in recent years.

## Getting to Ziro

There are four realistic ways in, and each has a catch.

| Route | Time to Ziro | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fly to Donyi Polo Airport (Hollongi, near Itanagar) | About 4 hours by road | Most travellers | Few daily flights; book early |
| Overnight train, Guwahati to Naharlagun | About 3 hours by road from Naharlagun | Budget travellers | Donyi Polo Express sells out for festival week |
| Fly to Dibrugarh | About 5 hours by road | Travellers combining Upper Assam | Longer drive, often in rain |
| Drive from Guwahati | About 9 hours | Groups with a car | Long day, landslide risk |
| Fly to Lilabari (North Lakhimpur) | About 3.5 hours by road | Short hops | Very limited schedule |

The road into the valley can be bumpy, and landslides are common during the rains. Leave a buffer day on both ends if you have a flight to catch. Shared taxis run from Naharlagun, and private taxis are easy to arrange in Itanagar; for festival week, we pre-book vehicles weeks in advance because demand spikes.

If you want to see more of the state capital on the way, stay a night in [Itanagar](/places/itanagar/) before driving up. It breaks the journey and gives you time to fix any e-ILP issues before the checkpoint.

## Where to stay during the festival

You have three options, and the right one depends on how much sleep you need.

- **Official camping partners.** The festival lists several camping operators each year, with pre-pitched tents, bedding and usually toilets and hot water nearby. Closest to the music, noisiest at night, and the easiest option for solo travellers.
- **Apatani homestays in the villages.** Quieter, warmer and the best way to understand the valley. You eat with a family, and your hosts often help with transport. Walking distance to the venue varies a lot, so ask.
- **Small hotels and guesthouses in Hapoli.** The main town has basic hotels and a market. You will need a vehicle or shuttle to reach the festival ground.

Whatever you pick, confirm in writing what is included: bedding, blankets, hot water, meals and transport to the venue. Late-September nights in Ziro can feel cold after rain, and a thin tent with no extra blanket makes for a long night.

## What to pack for Ziro in September

September in [Ziro Valley](/places/ziro-valley/) is the end of the monsoon. IMD normals put the average September high at about 24°C and the low at about 14°C, with around 200 mm of rain in the month. Expect showers and mud, with bright spells in between. Our list:

- Rain jacket and a small umbrella
- Waterproof shoes or gumboots; white sneakers will not survive
- A fleece or light down jacket for night sets
- Head torch, because paths back to camps are dark
- Power bank, since charging points are crowded
- Dry bags for phone, cash and permit printouts
- Reusable water bottle; the festival discourages single-use plastic
- Cash in small notes, as network and card machines are patchy

## Food at the festival and in Hapoli

Food stalls at the festival ground are largely run by local families and small businesses, and this is one of the best places in Arunachal to try Apatani and wider Arunachali food without hunting for it. Expect smoked pork and fish, bamboo shoot in many forms, local greens, sticky rice and plenty of chilli. Vegetarian options exist but are thinner, so vegetarians should ask stalls directly and carry some snacks.

Rice beer is part of the festival, served in bamboo mugs at many stalls. Drink water between cups; the altitude and a long day on your feet make it hit harder than you expect. In Hapoli, the market area has small restaurants and tea stalls that serve simple rice-and-curry meals, noodles and momos. For more on regional dishes, see our [Northeast food guide](/guides/northeast-food-guide/).

## Apatani culture and how to be a good guest

The Apatani are known for wet rice cultivation combined with fish farming in the same fields, and their landscape is on India's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage. Older women in the villages may still have the traditional facial tattoos and nose plugs, a practice that ended decades ago. Many visitors want photographs of them. Ask first, accept a no, and do not treat people as a backdrop.

Village paths cross private fields and bamboo groves. Stay on the paths, do not climb onto granaries, and ask before entering a house. Apatani rice beer is part of hospitality; accepting a small cup is polite, but no one will push you. The festival works because the community hosts it, so leave the valley cleaner than you found it.

## Going with friends, family or as a first-timer

Groups of four to six have the easiest time. One vehicle covers the airport or station transfer, and a homestay room or two tents keep everyone together. Families with teenagers do well in homestays, where there is a proper bed, a warm kitchen and hosts who know the roads.

If this is your first multi-day outdoor festival, pick a camp with a shuttle to the venue, and plan one quieter afternoon in the villages to recharge. Four days of rain, late nights and hill roads catch up with people on day three. Pace it, and you will still enjoy the closing night.

## Beyond the festival: two or three extra days

We rarely plan Ziro as a four-day trip. The valley deserves more, and the drive is long enough that a couple of extra days make sense.

- **Village walks:** Hong and Hija, with rice fields, bamboo houses and granaries.
- **Kile Pakho ridge:** a short hike for views across the whole valley.
- **Talley Valley Wildlife Sanctuary:** forest trails with a local guide, road permitting.
- **Onward to Pasighat or Mechuka:** for travellers with a week or more, [Pasighat](/places/pasighat/) and the Siang river are the gateway to the far north.

Ziro also has its own festivals outside September. Myoko in March and Dree in July are farming and community festivals, and both show a side of the valley the music festival does not. Our [Northeast festival calendar](/guides/northeast-festivals/) lists them alongside the other big events.
