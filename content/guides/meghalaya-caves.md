Switch off your torch 200 metres into Mawmluh and the dark is total. You hear water running somewhere below, a drip near your ear, and the guide's breathing. Then he clicks the lamp back on, and the wall beside you is streaked with calcite that took thousands of years to form.

Meghalaya sits on some of the most cave-riddled rock in Asia. Cavers have recorded well over a thousand caves here, and the longest known natural cave in South Asia runs under a ridge in the Jaintia Hills. Most visitors see one lit cave for 40 minutes. This guide covers that cave and what lies beyond it, with honest notes on who should go where.

## Why Meghalaya has so many caves

The southern edge of the Meghalaya plateau is limestone and sandstone that drops steeply toward the Bangladesh plains. It receives some of the heaviest rainfall on Earth, with Sohra and Mawsynram recording annual totals above 11,000 mm in many years. That water is slightly acidic, and over millions of years it has dissolved passages through the rock.

The result is cave country along a belt from the Garo Hills in the west, through the Khasi Hills around Sohra and Mawsynram, to the Jaintia Hills in the east. Local and international teams have mapped these caves every winter for more than three decades, notably through the Meghalayan Adventurers' Association and its long-running Caving in the Abode of the Clouds expeditions, which began in the early 1990s.

## Mawsmai: the easy one

[Mawsmai Cave](/places/mawsmai-cave/) is where nearly every Sohra day trip stops, and for good reason. The tourist route is roughly 150 metres, electrically lit, with a clear way in and out.

What to expect:

- A wide entrance, then a narrowing passage with stalactites and flowstone
- Two or three tight spots where adults turn sideways or crouch
- Wet floors throughout; many people go barefoot or in sandals
- Crowds from about 11 am, especially on weekends and in October and December

Go before 10 am for space to look properly. Entry is a small fee at the gate, and a camera fee may apply. The cave has closed on some days in extreme rain.

## Arwah: fossils and fewer people

Arwah, a short drive from Sohra market, starts with a paved walkway along the hillside with views across the valley. The cave itself is larger and more open than Mawsmai near the entrance and much quieter.

The reason to go is in the walls: marine fossils embedded in the limestone, a reminder that this rock formed under a sea. Allow 45–60 minutes, including the walk. It pairs well with Mawsmai on a single morning in [Sohra](/places/sohra/).

## Mawjymbuin: the cave with the stalagmite

Near [Mawsynram](/places/mawsynram/), Mawjymbuin is a short natural cave known for a large dome-shaped stalagmite under a steady drip of water. Many visitors come for that one formation, which has religious significance for some Hindu pilgrims.

Access is a short walk and a scramble over wet rock. It suits most reasonably fit visitors and takes about 30 minutes. Combine it with the drive through Mawsynram's rainfall country and the viewpoints on the road back.

## Mawmluh: a cave that defined an age

Mawmluh sits just outside Sohra town. At about 7.2 km, it is one of the longest caves in the Indian subcontinent. It is not a show cave: there is no lighting, the floor is wet, and parts require wading.

Its fame is scientific. In 2018, geologists defined the Meghalayan Age, the most recent stage of Earth's history, beginning about 4,200 years ago, using a stalagmite from Mawmluh. In 2022 the International Union of Geological Sciences named it one of its first 100 geological heritage sites.

We only take guests in with an experienced local caving guide, helmets and headlamps, and only between November and April. A two-to-four-hour trip goes deep enough to understand why cavers love this belt.

## Siju: bats and a river in the Garo Hills

Siju, in South Garo Hills beside the Simsang river, is one of the best-known caves in the west of the state. The entrance section is accessible with a guide; deeper passages involve water and are for experienced cavers. The cave is home to large bat colonies.

Siju is far from Shillong, so we fold it into a Garo Hills trip with [Balpakram National Park](/places/balpakram/). Our [Garo Hills and Balpakram](/packages/garo-hills-balpakram-5n/) route is built for this corner.

## Krem Liat Prah and the Jaintia Hills

The Shnongrim ridge in East Jaintia Hills holds about 150 known caves. Krem Liat Prah, at about 34 km, is recorded as the longest natural cave in South Asia, with a trunk passage cavers call the Aircraft Hangar.

It is not a tourist site. Exploring it means ropes, survey gear and days underground with a caving team. Around the ridge, and along the road east of [Jowai](/places/jowai/), village guides lead shorter trips into smaller caves; the quality of guiding varies a lot, so we arrange these only through people we have used before. Our [Jaintia Hills trip](/packages/jaintia-hills-4n/) can add a guided cave morning for fit travellers.

Another record belongs to Meghalaya too. Krem Puri, near Mawsynram, has been reported at about 24.5 km, the longest known sandstone cave in the world. Like Liat Prah, it is for expeditions only.

## What a guided cave trip looks like

A half-day trip into an undeveloped cave follows a pattern, whichever cave you choose.

1. **Briefing at the village:** the guide checks shoes, lights and fitness, explains the route and the turnaround time. Fees are paid here.
2. **Walk to the entrance:** anything from five minutes to an hour on village paths, sometimes through areca and betel plantations.
3. **Kit check at the mouth:** helmets on, headlamps tested, phones into dry bags.
4. **The cave itself:** expect crawling in places, wading to knee or waist height in some caves, climbing over boulders and long stretches of simply walking in the dark.
5. **Turnaround:** a good guide turns back at a set time regardless of how far you have got. That rule keeps people safe.
6. **Out and changed:** a hot tea in the village and a change of clothes.

Guides do not follow a standard fee, and the price depends on group size, cave and hours. We agree it in advance so there is no discussion at the cave mouth.

## Is caving in Meghalaya dangerous

The show caves are about as risky as a wet staircase. Slips and bumped heads are the main hazards, and a helmet or a little patience prevents most of them.

Undeveloped caves are different. The real risks are sudden water rise after rain, getting lost in a maze passage such as Mawmluh's, and cold after hours in wet clothes. None of these are a concern with a guide who knows the cave, a fixed turnaround time and the right season. They are serious without them. There is no cave rescue service on standby for tourists, which is one more reason to stay inside your experience level.

## Photographing underground

Phones struggle in the dark, and a few settings make a real difference:

- Use night mode and brace the phone against rock rather than hand-holding
- Ask a companion to light the scene from the side with a headlamp; front light flattens formations
- Keep flash away from bats and away from other people's eyes
- Wipe the lens often; humidity fogs it within minutes
- Bring a small tripod only for Arwah or Mawmluh; Mawsmai is too narrow and crowded

## Pressure on the caves

Meghalaya's caves face real threats. The same limestone that forms them is quarried for cement, and there are cement plants in the Sohra area and in East Jaintia Hills. Coal and limestone mining have damaged some cave systems, and plastic from roadside stops washes into others. Cavers have campaigned for years for stronger protection of the most important systems.

As a visitor, you help by choosing community-run caves, paying the village fee and leaving nothing behind.

## Cave rules and etiquette

Most of Meghalaya's caves are on community land governed by the village durbar and its headman. The rules we follow and ask our guests to follow:

1. **Pay the village fee and hire the local guide.** That money is often the village's only income from the cave.
2. **Do not touch formations.** Oil from skin stops calcite growth, and broken stalactites never grow back.
3. **No graffiti, no smoking, no litter.** Carry out every wrapper and bottle.
4. **Leave bats alone.** No flash directly at roosts and no shouting in bat chambers.
5. **Never enter an undeveloped cave without a guide,** and never alone.
6. **Turn back if it rains hard.** Water levels in river caves can rise in under an hour.
7. **Respect sacred sites.** Some caves and formations carry religious meaning; ask before photographing worshippers.

Meghalaya also requires accommodation providers to register every tourist, so carry ID. Our [Meghalaya travel rules guide](/guides/meghalaya-travel-rules/) covers fees and registration in full.

## When to go

November to April is caving season. Rain is low, water levels are down and access paths are dry. December to February is cool, around 8–15°C in Sohra, and the caves feel warmer than outside.

From June to September, the show caves near Sohra usually stay open, but we do not take anyone into undeveloped caves. Monsoon storms can flood passages fast. Our [best time to visit Meghalaya](/guides/best-time-to-visit-meghalaya/) guide covers the months in detail.
