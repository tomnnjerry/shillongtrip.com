The rules for visiting Meghalaya changed more between June 2025 and September 2026 than in the ten years before. The short version is still reassuring: you do not need a permit, and you do not pay to enter the state. But hotels now register every guest with the police, you cannot rent a self-drive car, and some treks and villages have their own conditions. We have rebuilt our own trip paperwork around these rules, and this is what we tell every guest before they land in Guwahati.

## The rules at a glance

| Rule | Applies to | In force since | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| No Inner Line Permit | Everyone | Always | Nothing to apply for |
| No statewide entry fee | Everyone | Always | Carry cash for small local fees |
| Guest registration on the state tourism app | All hotels, homestays, resorts, landlords | June 2025 | Show photo ID at check-in |
| Ban on self-drive rental cars | Tourists hiring private vehicles | June 2025 | Use a taxi with a driver |
| Registered guide for treks and outdoor activities | East Khasi Hills district | 30 June 2025 | Hire a guide at the trailhead or via your stay |
| Mawlynnong Sunday rule for day visitors | Mawlynnong village | January 2026 | Visit Monday to Saturday or stay overnight |
| Entry-point registration "on ILP lines" | All visitors | Announced July 2026; not live | Check current status before you travel |

## Why the rules changed

In May 2025, a tourist from Indore was murdered in the Sohra area during his honeymoon. The case made national news, and the state responded quickly. The cabinet revisited the Meghalaya Residents Safety and Security Act, the 2016 law that already allowed the state to register visitors, and within weeks the tourism and district administrations issued orders on guest registration, self-drive rentals and trekking guides.

At the same time, there is a long-running political debate in Meghalaya about an Inner Line Permit, similar to the ones in Arunachal Pradesh or Nagaland. The 2026 plan for registration at entry points is the government's response to that debate. For travellers, the result is more paperwork at hotels, and possibly at the border later, but still no permit to apply for in advance.

## Guest registration at hotels and homestays

Since June 2025, every accommodation in Meghalaya, from a Shillong hotel to a two-room homestay in Nongriat, must register guests on the state tourism app. The app is linked to the police, and the owner is liable if an unregistered guest is later involved in a crime. When the order came out, Tourism Minister Paul Lyngdoh said around 60% of accommodation units were already using the app; the rest were told to comply.

In practice, it is a two-minute step at check-in:

- Each adult shows a valid photo ID: Aadhaar, passport, voter ID or driving licence.
- The host enters your details, dates and sometimes your onward plan.
- Children are usually added with a parent's details.
- Foreign nationals show their passport and visa, and the host also files the standard Form C.

Carry the physical ID or a clear DigiLocker copy for every traveller. The most common hold-up we see is a family with only one adult's ID, which slows check-in at every stop.

## No self-drive rental cars

The June 2025 orders also banned the renting of private vehicles to tourists for self-drive. You can still bring your own car into Meghalaya, but you cannot pick up a self-drive rental in Guwahati or Shillong and drive yourself to Sohra or Dawki.

That is less of a loss than it sounds. The roads to [Sohra](/places/sohra/) and [Dawki](/places/dawki-umngot/) are narrow, foggy in the monsoon and short on parking at busy viewpoints. A local driver knows where to stop, which roads are blocked and how the parking works. In August 2026, the tourism department also said its app would add verified taxi bookings in a first phase, which should make fares easier to check once it launches.

## Treks need a registered guide

On 30 June 2025, the East Khasi Hills district administration made it mandatory for tourists to hire authorised, registered guides for treks and outdoor activities in the district. The district includes Shillong, Sohra and Mawlynnong, so the rule covers the most popular walks in the state, including the trail from Tyrna down to the [Double Decker Root Bridge at Nongriat](/places/nongriat-root-bridges/).

What this means on the ground:

- Guides are available at trailheads such as Tyrna, and through homestays and local operators.
- Fees are set locally, per group, and depend on the route and duration.
- Your guide is also your safety net: they know the river crossings, the swimming spots and the way back before dark.
- If you are staying overnight in Nongriat, your homestay host will usually arrange a guide for onward walks.

## Entry-point registration: what is planned

In July 2026, the state announced mandatory registration of tourists and visitors at entry points, described in the press as working "on ILP lines". The idea is that visitors will be checked in at facilitation centres as they enter Meghalaya.

Here is where things stood at the end of September 2026:

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| Umling, Ri-Bhoi (Guwahati–Shillong road) | Entry-exit point since December 2020; being expanded into a tourist facilitation centre |
| Ratacherra (eastern border with Assam) | Facilitation centre planned; land work in progress |
| Other border points | Further centres, including in Garo Hills, under discussion |
| Target opening | Officials said early December 2026 |
| Fee | None announced |
| Online pre-registration | No public portal announced |

The tourism minister has said publicly that tourists will not be harassed at the new centres, and that the government also wants tourists to use local guides more widely. Until the centres actually open and the process is published, nothing changes for you at the border. Check current status before you travel, especially for trips from December 2026 onwards.

## Village rules, fees and Sunday closures

Much of what you visit in Meghalaya is run by village councils and communities, not by the state. They set their own fees and sometimes their own rules.

- **Community fees:** Nohkalikai, Mawsmai Cave, root bridge trails and most viewpoints charge a small entry fee, typically under ₹100 per person, plus parking and sometimes a camera fee. Keep small notes.
- **Umngot boats at Dawki:** priced per boat by the local boat operators. Agree the fare and duration at the ghat before you step in.
- **Sundays:** the Khasi and Jaintia hills are largely Christian, and Sunday is a church day. [Mawlynnong](/places/mawlynnong/) has barred day visitors on Sundays since January 2026, and some other villages have similar rules. Shops and eateries in smaller places may be shut.
- **Cleanliness:** many villages ban plastic and littering. Carry a bag for your own waste.

## Taxis from Assam and local unions

Most visitors start in Guwahati, and many hire an Assam-registered taxi for the whole trip. That is legal, and these taxis run into Meghalaya every day. But there has been friction. In July 2024, members of a pressure group stopped Assam tourist taxis near Shillong, and ten activists were arrested; tourist traffic to Sohra and Dawki was briefly affected. In August 2026, the blockade ran the other way, with Assam transporters stopping Meghalaya-bound vehicles after the Shillong violence.

We handle this in two ways. For trips that stay mostly in Meghalaya, we use Meghalaya-registered vehicles and drivers based in Shillong. For longer routes that also cover Kaziranga or Tawang, we use drivers who do the Meghalaya circuit regularly and know which stands and parking areas expect local taxis. Either way, agree in writing who pays for parking, tolls and the driver's stay before you set off.

## Weather closures and monsoon orders

From June to September, heavy rain changes the rules on the ground. District administrations can close waterfalls, swimming spots, viewpoints and trekking routes at short notice when the India Meteorological Department issues heavy-rain alerts. Umngot boating at Dawki usually stops when the river rises and turns muddy, typically through the core monsoon months.

- Follow any closure notice at the site, even if the path looks passable.
- Do not swim in natural pools during or after heavy rain; water levels rise fast.
- Keep an extra half day in the plan for Sohra in the monsoon.
- Ask your driver or guide each morning which spots are open.

## Rules for foreign travellers

Meghalaya is one of the easier Northeast states for foreigners. There is no Protected Area Permit, no Inner Line Permit and no special registration beyond what applies across India. Hotels file Form C, and you should keep your passport with you on day trips.

The catch is the neighbouring states. If your route continues into Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Mizoram or Manipur, you will need a Protected Area Permit for each, arranged in advance through an operator. ## Strikes, blockades and staying informed

Most trips to Meghalaya run without a hitch, but local protests do happen. On 19 August 2026, a student rally in Shillong turned violent, and Assam-based transporters blocked Meghalaya-bound traffic near Guwahati for several days. Hotels in Shillong and Sohra reported mass cancellations, and vehicle movement resumed after the two state governments met on 22 August.

What we do when something like this happens:

- Watch local news and our drivers' updates the week before arrival.
- Keep a flexible first night, in Guwahati if needed.
- Avoid rally routes and large gatherings in Shillong.
- Stay with the driver's advice on timing and routes.
