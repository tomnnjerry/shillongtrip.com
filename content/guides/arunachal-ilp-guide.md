If your Tawang trip ends at the Bhalukpong gate, it is almost always because of one missing piece of paper. The Inner Line Permit is cheap, fully online and, since 2026, often approved in minutes, but the check post staff will not wave you through without it, and the nearest place to sort out a problem is back down the road in Tezpur.

We file permits for our Arunachal guests every week of the season. This page is the process as we run it under the 2026 rules: who needs which permit, what the portal asks for, what it costs, and where people get stuck.

## Who needs which permit

Arunachal Pradesh has been an "inner line" state since colonial times. The rule today is short: if you are not a resident, you need permission to enter. The type of permission depends on your passport.

| Traveller | Permit | Where to apply | Indicative cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indian citizen (tourist) | Temporary ILP, 15 days | eilp.arunachal.gov.in, online | About INR 300 to 500; pay what the portal shows |
| Indian citizen (longer stays) | Longer temporary ILP | Online, decided by the Deputy Commissioner | Set by the 2026 fee schedule |
| Foreign national, including OCI | Protected Area Permit (PAP) | Through an approved Indian tour operator | About USD 30 to 50 royalty per person |
| Citizens of Afghanistan, China, Pakistan | Prior clearance from the Ministry of Home Affairs | Ministry of Home Affairs | Case by case |
| Bum La visitors (Indian civilians only) | Additional border permit | DC office Tawang, stamped by the Army | Arranged locally |

Residents of Arunachal and some government staff on duty follow different rules. For a holiday, the first row is the one that matters.

## The 2026 Temporary ILP

Arunachal notified new **Inner Line Permit Guidelines in March 2026**. For tourists the headline changes are simple:

- You get a **Temporary ILP**, valid for **15 days** from the entry date you give.
- It covers **the whole state**, so you no longer need to list each district.
- Online applications authenticated with an **Aadhaar OTP** are **approved automatically**.
- The fee schedule sets **INR 300 for the 15-day permit**. Older pages on the same portal, and many travel sites, still show INR 300 for up to 3 days and INR 500 for longer stays. Budget about INR 300 to 500 per person and pay whatever the portal shows at checkout.

Fifteen days is enough for almost every itinerary we sell. Our [six-night Tawang trip via Dirang and Sela](/packages/tawang-6n/), from INR 29,000 per person, spends all six nights inside the state, and the [eight-night Western Arunachal circuit](/packages/west-arunachal-8n/), from INR 37,000, all eight. Only long combinations, such as Tawang, Ziro and Mechuka in one loop, come close to the limit, and for those we plan the dates first and file the permit second.

## How to apply online, step by step

The application takes about 15 minutes if your details are ready. The portal is eilp.arunachal.gov.in; ignore look-alike sites that add a "service fee".

1. **Start a tourist application** and verify your mobile number.
2. **Authenticate with Aadhaar** using the OTP sent to the number linked to your Aadhaar.
3. **Add a second photo ID**: voter ID, driving licence, passport or PAN card.
4. **Upload a recent photograph**: passport style, light background, face straight to the camera, no glare on spectacles.
5. **Enter trip details**: proposed entry date, itinerary, places of stay, a local contact in Arunachal and an emergency contact.
6. **Add family members** so everyone carries matching dates. For anyone under 18, the portal asks for a parent's or guardian's details.
7. **Pay online**, download the permit, print two copies and save the PDF on two phones.

A **registered tour operator** can file for groups of up to 50 people, which is how we handle our guests: we collect details on WhatsApp, file one group application and send back the permits. Even though approval is often instant, we file at least a week ahead, and two to three weeks ahead for October to November and March to May, because a mismatch with Aadhaar records can take days to fix.

### Details that cause problems

- **Aadhaar not linked to your current phone.** No OTP, no auto-approval. Fix this before the trip.
- **Name mismatch.** "S. Kumar" on one ID and "Suresh Kumar" on another slows the gate check. Use the full name everywhere.
- **Blurred photo or scan.** Take the photo against a plain wall in daylight.
- **Blank stay details.** Keep hotel names and your operator's local number ready.

## Where your permit is checked

Arunachal has around 71 check gates along its boundary with Assam, and your permit may also be checked at police posts inside the state. Carry the permit, on paper or phone, plus the original ID. Bhalukpong, Kimin and Pasighat have been reported to use QR scanners.

| Gate | Road | Leads to |
|---|---|---|
| Bhalukpong | Tezpur to Bomdila (NH13) | [Bomdila](/places/bomdila/), [Dirang](/places/dirang/), Sela and [Tawang](/places/tawang/) |
| Banderdewa | North Lakhimpur to Itanagar | [Itanagar](/places/itanagar/) and onward to Ziro |
| Kimin | Assam border north of Itanagar | The Ziro road |
| Seijosa | Assam border in Pakke-Kessang | Pakke Tiger Reserve |
| Ruksin | Towards Pasighat | Pasighat and the road north to Mechuka |
| Sunpura and Dirak | Eastern Assam border | Namsai and Tezu |

On the Tawang road, our drivers stop at Bhalukpong, hand over the permits with the original IDs, and the check usually takes 10 to 20 minutes. Queues form after 10 am on long weekends, which is one more reason we leave Tezpur early.

## At the gate: what to expect

The check itself is quick when the paperwork is right. The driver parks, one person from each car takes the permits and original IDs to the counter, and the staff match names, photos and dates. Sometimes they scan a QR code, sometimes they note details in a register. Keep children's permits with a parent.

On busy mornings, and especially on long weekends in October and April, several cars arrive at once and the wait stretches past 30 minutes. Police may check again at Bomdila, Dirang or the approach to Tawang town, so keep the permits handy rather than packed in a bag in the boot. On the way out, some gates check that you are leaving within your permit dates.

## Counters, extensions and penalties

If you cannot apply online, facilitation counters operate at **Guwahati's LGBI airport**, **Asom Paryatan Bhawan in Guwahati**, **Naharlagun and Gumto railway stations** and **Donyi Polo Airport in Itanagar**. Deputy Resident Commissioner offices in **Guwahati, Tezpur, Dibrugarh, Kolkata and Delhi** can also issue permits. Hours and queues vary, so the online route is still faster.

If plans change after approval:

- **Staying longer.** Apply online for an extension **at least 7 days before** the permit expires. Renewal costs the same as the permit.
- **Entering on a different date.** A permit with the wrong entry date can be questioned at the gate. If your flight slips by more than a day, file again; it is cheaper than a lost day.
- **Longer stays** for work or study are decided by the Deputy Commissioner, and the fee schedule sets separate rates for them.

Breaking permit conditions can bring a **INR 1,000 penalty**, and the Deputy Commissioner can bar a person from getting an ILP for up to two years. It is not a risk worth taking in a border state.

## Foreign travellers: the Protected Area Permit

Foreign nationals do not use the ILP portal; the portal itself directs them elsewhere. Arunachal falls under the Foreigners (Protected Areas) Order, 1958, so every foreign visitor needs a **Protected Area Permit**. OCI cardholders are treated as foreign nationals for this purpose.

How it works in practice:

- Apply through an **approved Indian tour operator**. Permits are issued through the Ministry of Home Affairs, the FRRO system, Indian missions abroad, or the Arunachal Home Commissioner or Secretary (Tourism) in Itanagar.
- **Groups of two or more** with a fixed itinerary are the standard case. Solo travellers have been reported as allowed only for Tawang, Bomdila and Ziro.
- The royalty is about **USD 30 to 50 per person**; your operator confirms the current figure.
- Validity has been reported as **30 days**, not extendable.
- Citizens of Afghanistan, China and Pakistan, and people of Pakistani origin, need prior clearance from the Ministry of Home Affairs.

Bum La is closed to foreign nationals even with a PAP. On 17 December 2024 the Ministry of Home Affairs also reimposed the PAP for Manipur, Mizoram and Nagaland, so a multi-state route needs planning state by state. ## Special permits beyond the ILP

- **Bum La Pass and Madhuri Lake.** Indian civilians only. Get a permit from the DC office in Tawang, then have it stamped at the Army cantonment, usually the day before, through your hotel or operator. Only SUVs go up, in clear weather. Our [Bum La permit guide](/guides/bum-la-pass-permit/) has the details, and the [seven-night Tawang trip with Bum La and Madhuri Lake](/packages/tawang-bumla-7n/), from INR 34,000 per person, builds in a spare day.
- **Protected forests.** Namdapha and other reserves charge their own entry and camera fees.

## Permits for the rest of the Northeast

One permit does not cover the region.

- **Nagaland, Manipur and Mizoram** each need their own ILP for Indian visitors. Nagaland's tourist ILP is INR 200 for 30 days, applied online.
- **Manipur** has a travel advisory in place; check current advisories and local conditions before planning a visit.
- **Meghalaya, Assam and Tripura** need no ILP. In Meghalaya, hotels register every guest on the state tourism app; our [Meghalaya travel rules guide](/guides/meghalaya-travel-rules/) explains how.
