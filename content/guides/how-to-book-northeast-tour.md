Two quotes for the same "7 nights Northeast" can differ by a five-figure sum per person, and both can be honest. The gap is almost always in the details that were not written down: which hotel, which car, which safari, and who pays when the road to Tawang closes for a day.

We have planned and fixed enough trips that went sideways elsewhere to know where the problems start. This guide is the checklist we would use if we were booking with someone else: what to get in writing, how to check the company, and how to pay without risk. It applies to Meghalaya, Assam, Arunachal and the rest of the Northeast.

## What the quote must say in writing

A good quote reads like a contract, not a brochure. Before you pay anything, you should have a document, not a WhatsApp message, that covers each of these.

| Item | What good looks like | What to push back on |
|---|---|---|
| Hotels | Named hotel for each night, room category, number of rooms | "Standard hotel", "3-star or similar" with no names |
| Meal plan | CP (breakfast), MAP (breakfast and dinner) or AP, per night | "Meals as per itinerary" |
| Vehicle | Model (Innova Crysta, Scorpio, Dzire), AC or non-AC, one car for the whole trip | "Suitable vehicle", or a change of car at each town |
| Route | Day by day, with night stops and approximate drive times | Only a list of places |
| Permits | Who applies for the eILP, PAP and Bum La permit, and if fees are included | "Permits extra" with no amount |
| Safaris and entries | Number of jeep safaris, range, and entry fees included or not | "Kaziranga sightseeing" |
| Transfers | Airport or station pick-up and drop with times | Missing return transfer |
| Exclusions | A clear list | "Anything not mentioned" alone |
| Price | Per person, twin sharing, with taxes shown | A single total with no breakdown |

"Or similar" is fine when the alternatives are named or clearly described. It is not fine when it hides a switch from a hotel in town to one 25 km out.

## Hotels: names, rooms and location

Ask for the hotel name and the exact room type for every night, and look each one up. In Shillong, check whether the hotel is in Police Bazar, Laitumkhrah or 10 km out on a highway. In Kaziranga, check whether the resort is near the range you are booked into. At [Tawang](/places/tawang/), ask whether rooms have heaters and hot water all day, since nights in winter drop well below freezing.

If the operator substitutes a hotel after you book, the contract should say it will be of the same category or better, and that you will be told before arrival, not at check-in.

## The vehicle question

Vehicles cause more complaints in the Northeast than hotels. Pin down four things:

1. **Model and seating.** A Dzire is fine for two people in Meghalaya. For four adults with luggage, you want an Innova Crysta or similar.
2. **At disposal or point to point.** "At disposal" means the car stays with you for sightseeing. Point-to-point transfers are cheaper but leave you to hire local taxis.
3. **Local union rules.** Some areas use local vehicles for certain stretches. Bum La from Tawang, for example, usually needs a locally registered taxi, and some Meghalaya sightseeing has had similar arrangements. The quote should say whether that cost is included.
4. **Terrain.** For the road over [Sela Pass](/places/sela-pass/) in winter, insist on an SUV with good ground clearance.

## Permits and bookings the operator should handle

The Northeast has more paperwork than most of India. Confirm in writing who does what.

- **Arunachal eILP (Indians):** applied for online before the trip. Many operators handle it with your ID; others ask you to apply. See our [Arunachal ILP guide](/guides/arunachal-ilp-guide/) for the process.
- **Arunachal eILP (groups):** under the 2026 rules, a registered tour operator can apply for a group of up to 50 people, which saves each traveller doing it separately.
- **Protected Area Permit (foreign nationals):** for Arunachal, applied for through an approved tour operator, usually for groups of two or more. Since December 2024, foreign nationals also need a PAP again for Nagaland, Manipur and Mizoram, so a multi-state trip needs the operator to plan every permit. Our [Northeast for foreign travellers](/guides/northeast-for-foreigners/) guide explains the rules.
- **Bum La permit:** issued in Tawang, usually a day ahead, with army clearance. It is for Indian nationals only; see [Bum La Pass](/places/bum-la-pass/).
- **Kaziranga safaris:** jeep safaris are booked by range and slot, and elephant rides run only at Kohora and Bagori, with limited seats. Ask how many safaris are included and in which ranges. Our [Kaziranga safari guide](/guides/kaziranga-safari-guide/) covers zones and timings.
- **Meghalaya registration:** since June 2025, hotels and homestays must register every guest on the state tourism app. You do not need a permit, but some sites charge small entry and camera fees. Self-drive rentals are prohibited in Meghalaya, so a package should include a car with driver.

Permit rules change, sometimes mid-season. A good operator tells you when they do; still, check current status before you travel.

## Cancellation and road-closure terms

This is the clause most people skip, and it matters most in the Northeast. Snow on Sela, a landslide on the Bhalukpong–Bomdila road, a flooded Kaziranga range or a closed Umngot boat ghat can all change a day.

Look for three things in the terms:

1. **Your cancellation.** A slab system is normal, for example a smaller deduction 30 or more days out, rising as the date approaches. The slabs should be written, with the deduction as a percentage of the package.
2. **Their cancellation or changes.** If the operator cancels, you should get a full refund or an equivalent alternative you accept.
3. **Force majeure and road closures.** The clause should say how they will reroute, what is refunded for unused services (for example a Bum La day that could not run), and who pays for an extra hotel night or vehicle day if you are held up. Expect to share or pay for extra nights caused by weather; what you want is the rate agreed in advance, not negotiated on a mountain.

If a trip includes Tawang, we always suggest one spare day at the end. It costs less than a missed flight.

## Red flags we see often

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| No hotel names until after payment | You cannot compare or check location |
| Payment requested to a personal account or UPI ID | Little recourse if something goes wrong |
| 100% advance months ahead | Leaves you exposed if the company folds or changes terms |
| No GST invoice or company name on the quote | Hard to verify who you are dealing with |
| Price far below every other quote | Usually smaller car, missing permits or unnamed hotels |
| Pressure to pay today for a "last seat" on a private tour | Private trips have no seats to sell out |
| No written cancellation or road-closure policy | Every problem becomes a negotiation |
| Contact only by WhatsApp, no office address | Nobody to find if the trip fails |

One red flag is not always proof of a bad operator. Two or three together should make you stop.

## How to check a registered operator

You do not need to be an investigator. Fifteen minutes is enough.

1. **Match the name.** The company name on the quote, the invoice and the bank account should be the same.
2. **Check GST.** Search the GSTIN on the government GST portal. It should be active and in the same legal or trade name.
3. **Look for tourism registration.** Ask whether the company is recognised by the Ministry of Tourism or registered with the relevant state tourism department, and ask to see the certificate. Not every small operator has central recognition, but it is a good sign when one does. For foreign nationals visiting Arunachal, Nagaland, Manipur or Mizoram, it matters more: the Protected Area Permit has to go through an approved operator, so an unregistered seller cannot get it for you.
4. **Find the office.** A real address that appears on maps, with photos and a working landline or number that someone answers.
5. **Read recent reviews carefully.** Look for reviews with trip details and photos across several months, not a burst of short five-star comments in one week.
6. **Talk to the planner.** Ask specific questions: how long is Dirang to Tawang in January, which Kaziranga range suits an afternoon safari. A real planner answers without hesitating.

## Paying safely

- Pay an advance of roughly 25–40%, not the full amount, unless the trip is days away.
- Pay only into the account named in the written quote, by bank transfer to a current account in the company's name or by card through a proper payment gateway.
- If someone sends "new" bank details by message after the quote, call the office on a number you already have before paying anything.
- Avoid personal UPI IDs, cash handovers to agents and payments to names you do not recognise.
- Ask for a receipt for every payment and a GST invoice for the package.
- Credit cards give you the right to raise a dispute if services are not delivered.
- Keep the written itinerary, terms and payment receipts together in one email thread.

## Ten questions to ask before you pay

Send these to every operator on your shortlist. The answers, and how quickly they come, tell you a lot.

1. Which hotel is booked for each night, and what room type?
2. Is the vehicle with us for the whole trip, and what model is it?
3. Who pays for the driver's food and stay, tolls, parking and state entry taxes?
4. Which permits do you apply for, and are their fees in the price?
5. How many Kaziranga safaris are included, in which ranges and at what times?
6. If Bum La or Sela closes on the day, what happens to that day's cost?
7. What is your cancellation schedule, in writing?
8. Which entry fees, boat rides and camera fees are included?
9. Who is our contact during the trip, and what number do we call at night?
10. Can you send a GST invoice in the company's name?

## Private trip or group departure

Most Northeast packages are private: your group, your car, your dates. A few operators run fixed group departures, which can be cheaper for solo travellers because the vehicle cost is shared. The trade-off is a fixed pace and fixed hotels. For a group departure, ask for the minimum group size, what happens if it does not fill, and whether the date can be cancelled at short notice. For a private trip, ask whether the price changes if one person drops out, since the car costs the same.

## Season, peak dates and price

The same package can cost noticeably more in peak weeks. In the Northeast those are usually the Durga Puja and Diwali holidays in October and November, Christmas and New Year, and summer school holidays in May and June. Kaziranga, Tawang and Shillong hotels fill first. If you are travelling then, book two to three months ahead and expect less flexibility on refunds, because hotels demand larger deposits. Off-season quotes can look cheap, but check that the places you want are actually open; Kaziranga's safaris, for example, stop during the monsoon.

## If something goes wrong on the trip

Raise it the same day, in writing, with the contact named in your booking. A WhatsApp message with a photo of the wrong room or the substituted car creates a record. Give the operator a fair chance to fix it on the spot; most problems, such as a room change or a replacement vehicle, can be solved within hours. Note any service you did not receive, such as a missed safari, and ask for the refund or adjustment to be confirmed before the trip ends. If the company stops responding after you return, your written quote, invoice and payment receipts are what a bank, card issuer or consumer forum will ask to see.

## Comparing two quotes fairly

Put the quotes side by side and line them up item by item. Convert both to per person, twin sharing. Mark every difference: hotel, room, meal plan, vehicle, safaris, permits, transfers.
