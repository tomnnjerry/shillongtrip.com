The first time most people see Tawang, they have been in a car for two days and crossed a 13,000 ft tunnel to get there. That journey is the trip. Guwahati to Tawang is not a transfer you sleep through; it climbs from the tea gardens of the Brahmaputra plain through three climate zones to a monastery town on a ridge facing the eastern Himalaya.

We run Tawang trips from March to May and October to December, plus snow departures in January and February. This guide covers the route, the permits, the months, the sights and the mistakes that cost people a day.

## The road from Guwahati

Everyone reaches Tawang on the same road: NH13, which leaves the Assam plains at Bhalukpong and climbs through West Kameng district. From Guwahati, the drive is about 450 to 555 km depending on where you start and which variant you take, and we split it over two days each way.

| Stretch | Approx. distance | Approx. time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guwahati to Tezpur | 180 km | 4 hrs | Plains, good highway |
| Tezpur to Bhalukpong | 60 km | 1.5 hrs | ILP check gate at the state border |
| Bhalukpong to Bomdila | 100 km | 4 to 5 hrs | Steep climb, landslide-prone in the monsoon |
| Bomdila to Dirang | 40 km | 1.5 hrs | Down into the Dirang valley |
| Dirang to Tawang | About 140 km | 5 to 6 hrs | Sela Tunnel or Sela Pass, then Jang |

Times assume normal conditions and a start after breakfast. Landslides from June to September and snow from December to February add hours. Bomdila to Tawang is about 171 km in total.

The standard plan is **Guwahati or Tezpur to Dirang (or Bomdila)** on day one and **Dirang to Tawang** on day two. The night at mid-height, around 1,500 m at Dirang or about 2,400 m at Bomdila, helps your body adjust before Tawang and Sela. Our [Guwahati to Tawang road guide](/guides/guwahati-to-tawang/) has the stop-by-stop detail.

### The Sela Tunnel and Sela Pass

The Sela Tunnel, opened in March 2024, runs at about 13,000 ft under the ridge below [Sela Pass](/places/sela-pass/). It saves roughly 10 km and an hour between Dirang and Tawang, and, more importantly, keeps the road open in winter when snow used to close the pass for days.

The old road over Sela Pass, at about 4,200 m, is still open in fair weather. It passes the frozen or half-frozen Sela Lake and the painted gate at the top. We take it on the way up when the sky is clear and the tunnel on the way down, or when snow closes the top. Our [Sela Pass and Sela Tunnel guide](/guides/sela-pass-guide/) covers both.

Beyond Sela, the road passes the Jaswant Garh war memorial and drops to Jang, where Nuranang Falls pours about 100 m off a cliff a short detour from the highway. Tawang is about an hour further.

### Flights and helicopters

Tezpur has the nearest airport with regular commercial flights, about 320 km from Tawang. Guwahati has far more flights. A government-backed helicopter service between Guwahati and Tawang has run in past years, but we could not confirm a current schedule. Treat it as subject to operation and check current status before you travel; we do not build itineraries around it.

## Permits for Tawang

Tawang is in Arunachal Pradesh, which is an inner line state. You need a permit before you reach the Bhalukpong gate.

- **Indian citizens** need an Arunachal Pradesh Inner Line Permit, applied online on the state eILP portal. Expect about INR 300 to 500 per person; pay whatever the portal shows. Carry a printout and the original ID you used.
- **Foreign nationals** need a Protected Area Permit, applied through an approved Indian tour operator, normally for groups of two or more. Solo foreign travellers have been allowed to Tawang, Bomdila and Ziro in some cases.
- **Bum La and Madhuri Lake** need an extra permit issued in Tawang, for Indian civilians only.

Our [Arunachal ILP guide](/guides/arunachal-ilp-guide/) walks through the application step by step. Permit rules in border districts change, so check current status before you travel.

## What to see in and around Tawang

### Tawang Monastery

[Tawang Monastery](/places/tawang-monastery/), founded around 1680 to 1681 by Merak Lama Lodre Gyatso, is the largest Buddhist monastery in India and the reason the town exists. The main assembly hall holds a tall gilded Buddha statue, and the complex includes a library, a museum and monks' quarters on a ridge above the valley. Go for the morning prayers, dress modestly and ask before photographing monks. A small museum fee is charged.

### Bum La Pass

[Bum La](/places/bum-la-pass/) sits at about 4,600 m on the India-China border, 37 km from Tawang on a rough mountain road. It is open to Indian civilians only, with a permit from the Deputy Commissioner's office in Tawang that is then stamped at the Army cantonment. You apply the day before through your hotel, driver or operator. Only SUVs go up, and only in clear weather; closures after snow are common from December to March. At the top, soldiers usually give visitors a short talk about the pass and its history; the visit is brief, photography is restricted near installations, and it is bitterly cold even in May. Start by 7 am and carry warm layers, water and snacks. Our [Bum La permit guide](/guides/bum-la-pass-permit/) covers the process.

### Madhuri Lake and the PT Tso lakes

Madhuri Lake, locally Sangetsar Tso, lies about 7 km past the Bum La turn-off and needs the same Tawang-issued permit. It is usually combined with Bum La in one long day, along with a stop at PT Tso and other high-altitude lakes on the way.

### In and near town

- **Tawang War Memorial**, a stupa-shaped memorial to soldiers of the 1962 war, with a small museum and evening light-and-sound show when it runs
- **Giant Buddha statue** on the hill above town
- **Urgelling Monastery**, associated with the birthplace of the sixth Dalai Lama
- **Nuranang Falls** near Jang, on the way in or out
- **The market**, for Monpa woollens, prayer flags and momos

## Monpa food and local customs

Tawang's food is closer to Tibet and Bhutan than to Assam. Look for:

- **Thukpa**, a noodle soup, and **momos**, steamed dumplings, in every small eatery
- **Zan**, a thick millet or maize dough eaten with curries in Monpa homes
- **Butter tea**, salty and rich, offered in monasteries and homestays
- **Churpi**, hard dried yak or cow cheese, sold in the market
- **Local spirits** made from millet or rice, offered at festivals

A few customs worth knowing: walk clockwise around stupas, prayer wheels and monasteries; remove shoes and hats inside prayer halls; do not point your feet at altars or monks; and ask before photographing people, especially at festivals. Many Monpa families speak Hindi, and English is common among younger people and hotel staff.

## Driving tips for the Tawang road

Almost every guest travels with a local driver in an SUV, and we recommend it. The road is well built in long stretches but changes quickly with weather.

- **Start early.** Aim to be moving by 7 am on both driving days; fog and cloud build on the passes after noon.
- **Expect Army convoys.** Military traffic has priority on NH13. Drivers pull over and wait.
- **Carry snacks and water.** Eateries exist at Bhalukpong, Tenga, Bomdila, Dirang, Sela and Jang, but long stretches in between have none.
- **Keep warm clothes in the car**, not in the boot. Sela can be below freezing even in April.
- **Refuel in Bomdila or Dirang** if you are self-driving your own car; pumps are few above Dirang.
- **Allow for landslide stops** from June to September, especially between Bhalukpong and Bomdila.

## Altitude, health and comfort

Tawang is high enough to affect people who come straight from sea level, and Sela and Bum La are higher still. Most healthy travellers are fine if they follow a few rules:

- **Sleep at Dirang or Bomdila** on the way up. Do not drive Guwahati to Tawang in one push.
- **Drink three to four litres of water** a day and go easy on alcohol for the first two nights.
- **Save Bum La** for your second or third day in Tawang, not the day you arrive.
- **Watch for warning signs**: a headache that does not go with rest, breathlessness at rest, confusion or vomiting. Descend and get help.
- **Carry your regular medicines**, and talk to a doctor first if you have heart or lung conditions.

Phone networks are patchy on the road and reasonable in town. ATMs exist in Tawang, Dirang and Bomdila, but carry enough cash for fees and small eateries.

## How we'd plan it

We build every Tawang trip around two driving days each way and one flexible day.

1. **Day 1: Guwahati or Tezpur to Dirang.** Cross the Bhalukpong gate before noon; reach Dirang by evening.
2. **Day 2: Dirang to Tawang via Sela.** Take the old pass road if the sky is clear; stop at Jaswant Garh and Nuranang Falls.
3. **Day 3: Tawang town.** Monastery at prayer time, the war memorial, the market. Your hotel files the Bum La permit.
4. **Day 4: Bum La and Madhuri Lake.** A full day, weather allowing.
5. **Day 5: Buffer.** Use it if Bum La was closed, or for Urgelling and a slow afternoon.
6. **Days 6 and 7: Back down** via Dirang or Bomdila to Tezpur or Guwahati.
