The Sohra Sumo leaves when it is full, not when the timetable says so. That one sentence explains most of how the Northeast moves. There are few trains in the hills, buses are slow, and app cabs thin out once you leave Guwahati. What fills the gap is the shared Sumo: a jeep-like SUV, usually 8 to 10 seats, running a fixed route for a fixed per-seat fare. Learn how it works and you can cross Meghalaya for a fraction of a private car.

## How a shared Sumo works

The name comes from the Tata Sumo, the vehicle that dominated these routes for decades. Today the fleet also includes Boleros, Scorpios and similar SUVs, but everyone still calls them Sumos.

1. **Find the counter.** Each route has a counter or a stand, often run by a drivers' association. There may be no big sign; ask a shopkeeper for the "Sohra Sumo" or "Dawki Sumo".
2. **Buy a seat.** You pay at the counter and get a seat number or your name on a list. Front seats go first and are the best for motion sickness.
3. **Wait for it to fill.** The Sumo leaves when every seat is taken. In the morning that can be 10 minutes; at noon it can be an hour.
4. **Luggage goes on the roof** under a tarpaulin. Keep valuables and a rain layer with you.
5. **Get off anywhere on the route.** Tell the driver where; you still pay the full fare in most cases.

If you want to leave immediately, you can buy all remaining seats. Couples and small groups sometimes do this to turn a shared Sumo into a private one for part of the price of a reserved taxi.

## Routes and stands from Shillong

Here are the routes most visitors use. Fares are indicative, based on recent traveller reports; they change with fuel prices, so confirm at the counter.

| Route | Where to board | Distance | Time | Indicative fare per seat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guwahati to Shillong | Paltan Bazar area, Guwahati | 100 km | 2.5–3.5 hours | Around ₹500 |
| Shillong to Guwahati | Police Bazar area, Shillong | 100 km | 2.5–3.5 hours | Around ₹500 |
| Shillong to Sohra | Iewduh (Bara Bazar) Sumo stand | 55 km | 1.5–2 hours | About ₹200–300 |
| Shillong to Dawki | Anjalee stand, near Bara Bazar | 82 km | 2.5–3 hours | A few hundred rupees |
| Shillong to Jowai | Ask at Iewduh or your hotel | 65 km | About 2 hours | Confirm at counter |

The Iewduh Sumo stand is a multi-storey building, and Sohra Sumos have traditionally used an upper floor. Counters inside Iewduh do shift from time to time, so ask your hotel the evening before.

## Shillong's local taxis

Inside [Shillong](/places/shillong/), small black-and-yellow taxis work in two ways:

- **Shared, on fixed routes.** Taxis run set routes such as Police Bazar to Laitumkhrah and pick up passengers along the way for a small per-seat fare. Wave one down and say your stop.
- **Reserved.** Tell the driver you want a "reserve" and agree the fare first. This is how you get to Ward's Lake, Don Bosco Museum or Shillong Peak with luggage or at an odd hour.

App-based cabs are not a dependable option in Shillong. Traffic is heavy in Police Bazar and on the Laitumkhrah road between 9 and 11 am and 4 and 7 pm; walking is often faster for short hops.

## Getting around Sohra and Dawki

A shared Sumo gets you to [Sohra](/places/sohra/), not around it. Nohkalikai, Mawsmai Cave, Seven Sisters Falls, Arwah Cave, Eco Park and the Tyrna trailhead for the root bridges are spread 5–15 km from Sohra market. Local taxis wait near the market and quote for a half-day or full-day circuit; agree the list of stops before you set off.

At [Dawki](/places/dawki-umngot/), the boat ghats are a short walk from the market and the bridge. Shnongpdeng is about 8 km upstream and Mawlynnong about 30 km back towards Shillong, both off the main road. Shared transport to them is irregular, so most travellers hire a taxi in Shillong for a Dawki–Mawlynnong day.

## Hiring a car with a driver

For most itineraries longer than two days, a car with a driver is worth it. You stop where you want, start at dawn for the light, and carry luggage between hotels without roof tarpaulins.

A few things to know in 2026:

- **Self-drive is out.** Since June 2025, Meghalaya has prohibited renting private vehicles to tourists for self-drive. Book a registered commercial vehicle with a driver.
- **Vehicle registration matters.** In August 2026, Meghalaya taxi associations protested against Assam-registered tourist cabs doing sightseeing inside the state, leading to disruption and cancellations. On 8 September 2026 the two state governments set up a joint panel to resolve grievances. Ask your operator whether your car is Meghalaya-registered or how the cross-state leg is handled, and check current status before you travel.
- **Car size.** Sedans are fine for two people on the Guwahati–Shillong highway. For Sohra, Dawki and Tawang, an SUV or MPV is more comfortable and handles broken stretches better.
- **Day length.** Drivers usually work from about 7 am to 6 pm. Night driving on hill roads is best avoided anyway.

## Guwahati and the rest of Assam

[Guwahati](/places/guwahati/) is the transport hub for the whole region, with the main airport and railway station. In the city, app-based cabs and auto-rickshaws are widely used, and city buses cover the main roads.

**To Kaziranga.** Buses from Guwahati towards Jorhat, Golaghat, Dibrugarh and Tinsukia use the highway past Kohora, the gateway to Kaziranga's Central range. Ask to be dropped at Kohora; the journey takes about 4.5–5 hours. The park itself is seen only by jeep or elephant safari, booked locally or through your lodge. It normally reopens in phases in October; check current status before you travel.

**To Majuli.** Travel to Jorhat by bus, train or car, then 13–14 km to Neamati Ghat. Government ferries cross to [Majuli](/places/majuli/) in about 1–1.5 hours and carry cars and bikes as well as passengers. Ferries run in daylight only and timings change with the river, so aim to reach the ghat by early afternoon. On the island, shared autos link Kamalabari and Garamur; hiring a scooter or a taxi for a day makes visiting the satras easier.

## Arunachal Pradesh

Arunachal has no trains into the mountains and long distances between towns, so road planning matters more here than anywhere else.

**Tawang.** The road runs from Guwahati or Tezpur through Bhalukpong (the ILP check gate), Bomdila, Dirang and the Sela tunnel to [Tawang](/places/tawang/). Guwahati to Tawang is roughly 450–555 km depending on the route. Shared Sumos run from Tezpur, and less often Guwahati, in one very long day of 10–12 hours or more; state transport buses also run on parts of the route. We strongly suggest breaking the journey at Dirang or Bomdila both for comfort and for altitude.

**Ziro and Itanagar.** Itanagar has Donyi Polo Airport and Naharlagun railway station, with trains from Guwahati. Shared Sumos run from Itanagar and Naharlagun to Ziro via the Kimin or Banderdewa gates in about 4–5 hours.

**Permits.** Indian citizens need an Arunachal ILP; tourist permits cost about ₹300–500 per person depending on duration, so pay whatever the official portal shows. Our [Arunachal ILP guide](/guides/arunachal-ilp-guide/) explains the steps.

## From Guwahati Airport

Guwahati's Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi International Airport sits about 28 km west of the city centre, and its new terminal opened in December 2025. That location matters: the airport is on the far side of the city from the Shillong road, so allow 3–3.5 hours from the airport to Shillong in a taxi, compared with 2.5–3.5 hours from Paltan Bazar.

You have three choices on landing:

- **Reserved taxi from the airport** straight to Shillong. The simplest option with luggage, and the one we use for guests.
- **Taxi or app cab into the city,** then a shared cab from Paltan Bazar. Cheaper, but adds an hour or more.
- **Airport shared cabs to Shillong,** which are sometimes offered at the arrivals area. Confirm the drop point and the fare per seat before loading bags.

If your flight lands after about 2 pm in winter, consider a night in Guwahati. The last hour of the Shillong road in the dark, with trucks and fog, is the least pleasant drive of the trip.

## Nagaland and the other states

**Kohima** is about 3 hours by road from Dimapur, which has the nearest railway station and airport. Shared taxis and buses run between the two through the day. Indian citizens need a Nagaland ILP, applied for online. During the Hornbill Festival from 1 to 10 December, the festival ground at Kisama is about 12 km from Kohima, and taxis and shared vehicles do the run every day; see our [Hornbill Festival guide](/guides/hornbill-festival-guide/).

**Aizawl** is best reached by flying to Lengpui, about 30 km away, and **Agartala** by flying from Guwahati or Kolkata. Road journeys to both are long and are best left to travellers with plenty of time.

## Flights, trains and helicopters

**Flights.** Most visitors fly into Guwahati. Shillong's Umroi airport, about 30 km from the city, handles small aircraft on a handful of routes, including IndiGo to Kolkata, Dibrugarh and Imphal and Alliance Air to Guwahati, Aizawl, Dimapur, Tezpur and Lilabari. Schedules change often, so check before you plan around them. Itanagar, Dibrugarh, Jorhat, Tezpur, Dimapur, Imphal, Agartala and Aizawl all have airports.

**Trains.** There is no passenger railway to Shillong; the nearest station is Guwahati, 100 km away. Trains are useful for Guwahati to Jorhat (for Majuli), Dimapur (for Kohima, about 3 hours by road), Naharlagun (for Itanagar and Ziro), Dibrugarh and Agartala.

**Helicopters.** Government-backed helicopter services have linked Guwahati with Shillong and with Tawang at various times, using small aircraft with limited seats and strict baggage limits. As of September 2026 we could not confirm a current bookable schedule for either route. Treat them as subject to operation, check current status directly with the operator, and never buy from a website you found through an advert.

## Which mode suits which trip

| Mode | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Shared Sumo | Budget and solo travel between towns | Morning-only departures, cramped seats, roof luggage |
| MTC or ASTC bus | Lowest cost on the Guwahati–Shillong and Assam highway routes | Slower, fewer departures |
| Local taxi, reserved | Sightseeing loops around Sohra, Dawki and Shillong | Agree fare and stops first |
| Car with driver | Multi-day trips, families, photography, Tawang | Vehicle registration issues across states |
| Train | Guwahati to Jorhat, Dimapur, Naharlagun, Dibrugarh | No rail in Meghalaya's hills |
| Flights | Long hops such as Guwahati to Dibrugarh or Aizawl | Small aircraft, weather delays |

## Tips from our drivers

- Be at the Sumo stand by 7–8 am. After 10 am you may wait an hour.
- Carry cash in small notes; counters rarely take cards and networks drop.
- Sunday services in Meghalaya are lighter because it is a day of rest for many.
- Take the front seat or a window if you get car sick, and eat a light breakfast.
- Keep your rain jacket and ID out of the roof luggage.
- In the monsoon, road closures can cancel departures; our [Meghalaya monsoon guide](/guides/monsoon-in-meghalaya/) covers recent closures.

## How we'd plan it

For a budget trip, we mix shared transport with one or two reserved days, which keeps costs low without wasting hours waiting at stands:

1. Shared cab from Guwahati to Shillong.
2. Shared Sumo to Sohra, then a local taxi for a half-day of viewpoints.
3. One reserved day from Shillong for Dawki, Shnongpdeng and Mawlynnong.
4. Shared cab back to Guwahati.
