Stand in the arena at Kisama on the morning of 1 December and you will hear the log drum before you see anything. The Naga tribes, each in its own dress and ornaments, move into the ring in turn. That one week draws more visitors to Nagaland than most other months combined, and it is only one of dozens of festivals across the eight states.

The Northeast's festival year follows rice. Sowing, first harvest and the main harvest each have their celebrations, and every community marks them differently. This calendar covers October 2026 to September 2027, with the festivals we actually plan trips around.

## How dates work here

Before the calendar, one warning: many Northeast festivals do not have fixed dates.

- **Fixed dates:** Hornbill (1–10 December), Rongali Bihu (from 14 April), Magh Bihu (Uruka on 14 January), Me-Dam-Me-Phi (31 January), Moatsu (1–3 May), Dree (5 July), Solung (1 September).
- **Lunar or religious calendars:** Durga Puja, Raas Mahotsav, Losar, Torgya, Holi and Yaoshang shift each year.
- **Set by traditional authorities:** Behdienkhlam, the Nongkrem Dance and Wangala are fixed by clan or village leaders, sometimes only a few weeks ahead.

For anything in the last two groups, we hold hotel rooms with a flexible date and confirm once the organisers announce. Check current status before you travel.

## October to December: the peak season

This is when we would send anyone who wants festivals and good weather together.

**Durga Puja** fills Guwahati, Shillong and Agartala with pandals for five days in mid-to-late October 2026. Roads in Guwahati jam in the evenings; Shillong's Puja is gentler.

**The Nongkrem Dance** at Smit, about 15 km from [Shillong](/places/shillong/), is a thanksgiving festival of the Hima Khyrim, the Khasi chiefdom based there. Unmarried women dance in heavy gold and silver ornaments while men dance with swords and whisks. The dates are set by the chiefdom, usually in October or November.

**Wangala**, the Garo post-harvest festival, is celebrated in villages from September to December, and the state-level 100 Drums festival at Asanang, about 18 km from Tura, is usually held over three days in November. Hundreds of dancers move in lines to the beat of long drums.

**Raas Mahotsav** on [Majuli](/places/majuli/) is a three-to-five-day festival of performances about the life of Krishna, staged by the Vaishnavite satras around the Kartik full moon. In 2026 that falls in late November. Majuli has few rooms, so book early; our [Majuli, Sivasagar and Jorhat](/packages/majuli-heritage-6n/) trip can be timed to it.

**The Hornbill Festival** at Kisama, about 12 km from [Kohima](/places/kohima/), runs from 1 to 10 December every year and has since 2000. Each tribe has its own morung, or traditional house, with food, crafts and performances. Our [Hornbill Festival guide](/guides/hornbill-festival-guide/) covers tickets, stays and the Night Carnival, and our [Hornbill Festival, Kohima](/packages/hornbill-festival-5n/) trip includes Khonoma.

**Christmas** in Shillong, Kohima and Aizawl is a major community event, with carol singing and lit streets from mid-December.

## January to March: winter and new year

**Magh Bihu** is Assam's harvest feast. On Uruka night, 14 January, communities build temporary huts called bhelaghar and feast together; on the morning of the 15th the meji bonfire is lit. Food is the centre of it: pitha, jolpan and fish.

**Torgya** at Tawang Monastery is a three-day monastic festival with masked cham dances in the monastery courtyard, usually in January. **Losar**, the Tibetan New Year, follows in February or early March; it fell on 18 February in 2026. Both are cold, often snowy and quiet apart from the festival itself. [Tawang](/places/tawang/) hotels fill fast; our [Northeast in Winter](/packages/northeast-winter-8n/) route pairs Tawang with Kaziranga and Shillong.

**Sekrenyi**, the Angami purification festival, is held in late February in villages around Kohima. **Chapchar Kut**, Mizoram's spring festival, fills Aizawl in early March with the Cheraw bamboo dance. **Myoko**, the Apatani spring festival, rotates among villages in [Ziro Valley](/places/ziro-valley/) in late March.

## April to June: spring and Bihu

**Rongali Bihu** is Assam at its most joyful. From 14 April, towns and villages hold Bihu dances, with dhol drums, pepa buffalo-horn pipes and young dancers in muga silk. Guwahati sets up stages for about a week. Our [Rongali Bihu in Assam](/packages/bihu-assam-5n/) trip spends it in Guwahati, Sivasagar, Jorhat and Majuli, where the village Bihu is more personal than the city stages.

**Shad Suk Mynsiem**, the Khasi festival of joyful hearts, takes place over three days in April at the Weiking ground in Shillong.

**Aoleang** in Mon district (1–6 April) and **Moatsu** in Mokokchung (1–3 May) are the spring festivals of the Konyak and Ao Nagas.

**Ambubachi Mela** at [Kamakhya Temple](/places/kamakhya-temple/) in Guwahati, in late June, draws very large crowds of pilgrims. The temple doors stay closed for three days and reopen on the fourth; in 2026 they were closed from 23 to 25 June and reopened on the 26th. If you are not a pilgrim, avoid Guwahati's Nilachal Hill that week.

## July to September: the monsoon festivals

**Behdienkhlam** in [Jowai](/places/jowai/) is the Pnar festival to drive away disease, held in July after sowing. Men carry tall, decorated wooden structures and wrestle with logs in a pool of muddy water. The dates are set by the dolois, the traditional leaders, and announced ahead of each year's festival. Our [Jaintia Hills trip](/packages/jaintia-hills-4n/) can be timed to it.

**Dree**, on 5 July in Ziro, is the Apatani agricultural festival. **Kharchi Puja** in Agartala worships the fourteen deities in July. **Solung**, the Adi harvest festival, starts on 1 September around Pasighat.

The **Ziro Festival of Music** is the one contemporary festival in this list. The 2026 edition ran from 24 to 27 September, and the next is expected in late September 2027, with dates announced by the organisers; see our [Ziro Festival of Music guide](/guides/ziro-festival-of-music/) and the [Ziro Festival of Music trip](/packages/ziro-festival-5n/).

## Getting to the big venues

The headline festivals are all reachable from a regional airport, but the last stretch matters.

- **Hornbill:** fly to Dimapur or Guwahati. Dimapur to Kohima is about 70 km, roughly 3 hours; Kisama is another 12 km beyond Kohima. Shared taxis run to the venue every day of the festival.
- **Rongali Bihu:** Guwahati airport is 20–25 km from the city stages. Village Bihu around Sivasagar and Jorhat is best reached from Jorhat airport.
- **Wangala:** Tura has limited flights, so most visitors drive from Guwahati, about 220 km and 6–7 hours via Goalpara.
- **Losar and Torgya:** Guwahati to Tawang takes two driving days, about 450–520 km, with a night in Dirang or Bomdila.
- **Behdienkhlam:** Jowai is 64 km from Shillong, about 1.5–2 hours, so a day trip works.

## Smaller festivals worth a detour

Beyond the headline events, these are the ones we build into trips when dates line up. None needs a ticket, and all are community events first.

- **Me-Dam-Me-Phi (31 January):** the Ahom ancestor-worship festival, marked with public prayers in Sivasagar and other Upper Assam towns.
- **Ali-Ai-Ligang (February or March):** the Mising sowing festival on Majuli and along the Brahmaputra, with the Gumrag dance and rice beer.
- **Nyokum (26 February):** the Nyishi festival for prosperity, celebrated across central Arunachal.
- **Mopin (5 April):** the Galo festival in Aalo, known for rice-flour smeared on faces as a blessing.
- **Kut (1 November):** the Kuki-Chin-Mizo harvest festival in Manipur, when conditions allow.
- **Garia Puja (April):** a Tripuri festival worshipping Garia, the god of livestock and wealth.
- **Sangken (13–16 April):** the Khamti and Singpho water festival around Namsai in eastern Arunachal.

A traditional festival in a small town can be the best day of a trip, but it is not a show. Timings slip, programmes change and speeches can run long. Go with patience.

## Festival etiquette

The festivals in this calendar belong to living communities. A few habits keep you welcome:

1. **Ask before photographing people,** especially elders in traditional dress and anyone taking part in a ritual.
2. **Never step into a performance space** or a sacred area unless invited.
3. **Keep drones grounded** unless the organisers have given permission; Hornbill and many village festivals restrict them.
4. **Buy from the stalls.** Crafts and food sold at festivals support the families who make them.
5. **Accept food and rice beer politely,** but it is fine to decline. A small taste is always enough.
6. **Dress modestly** at church services, satras and monasteries, and remove shoes where asked.

## Permits and practical notes

| Festival state | Indians | Foreign nationals |
|---|---|---|
| Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura | No permit | No permit; hotel Form C |
| Arunachal (Losar, Torgya, Ziro, Solung) | Arunachal eILP | Protected Area Permit |
| Nagaland (Hornbill, Sekrenyi, Moatsu) | Nagaland ILP | PAP; Hornbill 2025 was relaxed temporarily |
| Manipur (Sangai, Yaoshang) | Manipur ILP | PAP; check the current travel advisory |
| Mizoram (Chapchar Kut) | Mizoram ILP | PAP, plus police registration within 24 hours |

Rules for foreign visitors changed in December 2024 and may change again. Check current status before you travel.

A few practical points:

- **Crowds:** Hornbill, Rongali Bihu in Guwahati and Ambubachi draw the biggest crowds. Village festivals are far smaller.
- **Photography:** ask before photographing anyone in ritual dress, and follow organisers' rules on flash and drones.
- **Dress:** modest clothing is appropriate at religious events. Remove shoes where asked.
- **Weather:** Hornbill nights drop to around 5–8°C in Kohima; Losar in Tawang can be below freezing.
