The first time we drove this road with guests, the only thing that went wrong was the start time: we left Guwahati at 8 am and reached Dirang in the dark. Every trip since has left by 6. That one change makes the whole journey feel different.

This guide covers the road itself: distance by section, which overnight stop to choose, what a taxi really costs in 2026, the cheaper shared options, permits at Bhalukpong and how the season changes each day. For what to do once you arrive, read our [Tawang travel guide](/guides/tawang-travel-guide/) and the day plans in [Tawang itinerary](/guides/tawang-itinerary/).

## The route at a glance

There is one main road into Tawang from the plains. From [Guwahati](/places/guwahati/) you run east on the highway towards [Tezpur](/places/tezpur/), turn north at Balipara, enter Arunachal Pradesh at Bhalukpong and climb through West Kameng on NH-13 to [Bomdila](/places/bomdila/), [Dirang](/places/dirang/), the Sela Tunnel and finally [Tawang](/places/tawang/). Some drivers skip Tezpur town by taking the Mangaldoi–Orang road straight to Balipara, which saves 20–30 km.

| Section | Approx. distance | Driving time (dry season) | What it is like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guwahati to Bhalukpong | 210–240 km | 5–6 hrs | Flat highway, trucks, dhabas at Tezpur and Balipara |
| Bhalukpong to Bomdila | 95–105 km | 3.5–4.5 hrs | Steady climb, Nechiphu Tunnel, slide-prone in rain |
| Bomdila to Dirang | 40–45 km | 1.5 hrs | Down into the Dirang valley, apple and kiwi orchards |
| Dirang to Tawang | 125–135 km | 4.5–6 hrs | Sela Tunnel, Jang, Nuranang, steep final climb |
| **Total** | **about 450–555 km** | **14–16 hrs** | Almost always split over two days |

Distances vary between maps because the road keeps being realigned. The Nechiphu Tunnel (about 500 m, opened in 2023) bypasses a foggy stretch below Bomdila, and the Sela Tunnel shortened the Dirang–Tawang leg by roughly 10 km. Treat any single figure you read, including ours, as a range.

## Altitude on the way up

This is the part people underestimate. You start near sea level and sleep at around 3,000 metres two days later.

- Guwahati: about 55 m
- Bhalukpong: about 200 m
- Bomdila: about 2,400 m
- Dirang: about 1,500 m
- Sela Tunnel: about 13,000 ft, roughly 4,000 m (the old pass road tops out near 4,170 m)
- Tawang town: about 3,000 m

Dirang being lower than Bomdila is the main reason we prefer it for the night stop. You climb high, drop down to sleep, then climb again the next morning. That pattern suits the body better than sleeping at 2,400 m on night one.

## Dirang or Bomdila for the night

We get this question more than any other on this route. Both work; they just shape the two days differently.

| | Night in Dirang | Night in Bomdila |
|---|---|---|
| Altitude | about 1,500 m | about 2,400 m |
| Day one from Guwahati | 9–11 hrs, 330–380 km | 8–10 hrs, 290–340 km |
| Day two to Tawang | 4.5–6 hrs | 6–7.5 hrs |
| Good for | Families, seniors, first-timers | Late Guwahati arrivals, cold-lovers |
| Evening | Hot springs, dzong village, riverside walk | Monastery above town, market |
| Winter | Mild nights | Freezing nights, frost on roads at dawn |

If your flight reaches Guwahati after 11 am, you will not make Dirang in daylight. In that case, sleep in Tezpur or Bhalukpong on night zero, or stop at Bomdila and accept a longer second day. The slower trip in our [Dirang and Bomdila package](/packages/dirang-slow-5n/) is built around exactly this: two nights in the valley before anyone goes near 3,000 m.

## Day one, hour by hour

Here is how a well-run first day usually goes, leaving Guwahati at 6 am in November.

- **6:00** Leave Guwahati. Roads are clear until Baihata Chariali.
- **8:30–9:00** Breakfast at a dhaba near Tezpur or on the Orang road.
- **10:30–11:00** Bhalukpong check gate. Permits and ID are checked here; queues can take 20–40 minutes in peak season.
- **11:30** Tipi Orchid Centre stop if you have the time (about 5 km in).
- **13:30** Lunch near Rupa junction or Tenga.
- **15:30** Bomdila. Tea stop, short walk to the monastery above town.
- **17:00** Dirang, before dark in most months.

In June to September add 1–3 hours for rain and slide clearance between Bhalukpong and Bomdila. In December, dusk falls by 4:30 pm, which is another reason for the early start.

## Day two over the Sela Tunnel

Leave Dirang by 7:30 am. The road climbs steadily to the Sela approach, where you choose between the tunnel and the old road over [Sela Pass](/places/sela-pass/). Most drivers take the tunnel when the pass is snowbound, and the pass road when it is clear and you want the lake and the gate. Our [Sela Pass guide](/guides/sela-pass-guide/) covers that choice in detail.

After the tunnel the road drops to Jang, where the side road to [Nuranang Falls](/places/nuranang-falls/) is worth 45 minutes, then climbs again to Tawang. You should reach town by 1–2 pm, which leaves the afternoon for rest. We keep sightseeing, including [Tawang Monastery](/places/tawang-monastery/), for the next morning, after a night at altitude.

## Taxi fares by vehicle

Almost everyone reaches Tawang in a private vehicle hired with a driver. Arunachal drivers and Arunachal-registered vehicles are common on this route; some Guwahati taxis do not go beyond Bomdila, so ask before you book.

| Vehicle | Seats (comfortable) | One-way, 2 days (INR) | Round trip, 6 days with sightseeing (INR) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sedan (Dzire, Etios) | 3 | 15,000–19,000 | Not advised beyond Dirang in winter |
| Bolero / Scorpio / Xylo | 5–6 | 18,000–26,000 | 45,000–60,000 |
| Innova Crysta | 5–6 | 24,000–32,000 | 55,000–75,000 |
| Tempo Traveller (12 seat) | 10–12 | 32,000–42,000 | 80,000–1,10,000 |

All fares are indicative for 2026 and include fuel, tolls, driver allowance and the driver's night stay. They move with fuel prices and season: October, the Diwali weeks and late December are the most expensive. Parking at viewpoints and any Bum La local vehicle charge are usually extra. For a full costing of stays, meals and permits, see [Tawang trip cost](/guides/tawang-trip-cost/).

We do not recommend a sedan between December and March. Ground clearance matters on broken sections below Sela, and snow chains fit better on SUVs.

## Shared Sumos and buses

If you are travelling solo or on a tight budget, shared Tata Sumos and similar SUVs run the route every day. They leave Tezpur and Guwahati very early, often between 5 and 6 am, and fill up the night before in peak months. Fares reported by travellers have ranged from about INR 1,300 (Tezpur to Tawang) to INR 1,700–2,000 (Guwahati to Tawang) per seat, with the Dirang to Tawang leg around INR 700–900. Expect those numbers to have risen a little by 2026.

Tips if you go shared:

- Book the front seat a day ahead if you get car-sick; it is worth paying extra.
- Keep your eILP printout and ID in your day bag, not on the roof.
- Break the trip in Dirang or Bomdila and catch the next morning's Sumo onward.

A state-run bus has also operated between Guwahati and Tawang, taking about 16–17 hours in one push. It is cheap but tiring, and schedules change. Check current status before you travel.

## Trains and flights

There is no train to Tawang. The nearest useful rail hubs are Guwahati and the Rangapara–Tezpur area, and from either you continue by road. Tezpur's small airport has limited flights; most visitors fly into Guwahati.

An Arunachal government helicopter service began flying between Guwahati and Tawang in 2014. As of late 2026 we could not find a current bookable schedule or fare for that sector, so we do not sell it and do not plan trips around it. If a service is running when you travel, treat it as a bonus and keep the road plan as your backup. Check current status before you travel.

## Permits at Bhalukpong

Arunachal Pradesh is an Inner Line state. Indian citizens need a tourist Inner Line Permit, applied for online through the state eILP portal with Aadhaar authentication, your itinerary and your places of stay. Under the 2026 rules the tourist permit is a Temporary ILP valid for 15 days; expect to pay about INR 300–500 per person, and pay whatever the portal shows. It is checked at the Bhalukpong gate, which is reported to use QR scanners, and you may be asked again near Tawang. Carry the eILP on your phone or in print, plus the original photo ID you used to apply.

Foreign nationals need a Protected Area Permit, applied for through an approved tour operator, usually for groups of two or more, with a royalty of about USD 30–50 per person that your operator will confirm. Solo foreign travellers have been reported as allowed for Tawang and Bomdila. Rules change, so check current status before you travel. Bum La Pass needs an additional permit from the DC office in Tawang, stamped at the Army cantonment, and it is open to Indian nationals only. Our [Arunachal ILP guide](/guides/arunachal-ilp-guide/) walks through the form, the fee and the documents.

## Season by season

| Months | Road conditions | Our advice |
|---|---|---|
| March–May | Mostly dry, clear skies, rhododendrons near Sela | Best all-round window |
| June–September | Heavy rain, landslides between Bhalukpong and Bomdila | Add a buffer day; avoid if you have a flight to catch |
| October–mid-December | Dry, crisp, best views of the Kameng valley | Busiest; book vehicles early |
| Mid-December–February | Snow at Sela, icy mornings, tunnel keeps road open | SUV only, keep one spare day |

The Sela Tunnel changed winter travel more than anything else in the last decade. Before it opened, a single heavy snowfall could shut Tawang in for days. Now closures are shorter, but they still happen, and slides below Bomdila in the monsoon are unaffected by the tunnel. For snow trips, our [winter Tawang package](/packages/tawang-snow-6n/) builds in the slack.

## Food, fuel and phone signal

Plan meals around towns, not viewpoints. Tezpur, Balipara, Bhalukpong, Tenga, Bomdila, Dirang, Jang and Tawang all have simple places serving rice, dal, thukpa and momos. Between Bomdila and Dirang, and across the Sela stretch, there is very little, so carry water, biscuits and fruit in the car. The army-run cafeteria near Sela and small tea stalls at Jang are open most days in season, but do not count on them in heavy snow.

Fuel up in Tezpur or Bhalukpong, then again in Bomdila or Dirang. Your driver will know the pumps, but on a self-drive trip never let the tank drop below half above Bomdila.

Mobile coverage is patchy. Expect usable signal in Tezpur, Bhalukpong, Bomdila, Dirang and Tawang town, and long gaps in between, especially around Sela. Postpaid connections on the larger networks tend to work better than prepaid ones from other states. Download offline maps, save your hotel numbers and send your driver's number to someone at home before you leave Guwahati.

## What to carry in the car

Even in May, the temperature at Sela can be near freezing while Guwahati is at 30 °C. Keep these within reach, not packed in the boot:

- A fleece or down jacket, cap and gloves for the Sela section
- Motion-sickness tablets; the Bhalukpong–Bomdila climb has hundreds of bends
- Your eILP or PAP (on the phone and in print) and the original ID used to apply, plus two photocopies
- Cash of at least INR 3,000–5,000 per person; ATMs exist in Bomdila, Dirang and Tawang but can run dry on holiday weekends
- Sunglasses and sunscreen for snow glare in winter
- A thermos; many hotels will fill it with hot water in the morning

## Coming back down

The return from Tawang follows the same road, and it is not much faster. Leave Tawang by 7 am, cross Sela before the afternoon cloud comes in, and sleep in Dirang or Bomdila again. From there, Guwahati is another 9–11 hours, so an evening flight on the last day works only if everything goes right. We suggest booking your flight home for the day after you reach Guwahati, or a late-afternoon flight from Tezpur if one is running that week. Travellers heading on to wildlife parks can stop at Nameri or Kaziranga on the way down instead of going back to Guwahati first.

## Mistakes we see every season

1. **Leaving Guwahati late.** Anything after 7 am in winter puts you on mountain roads after dark.
2. **Going straight to Tawang with no stop.** People arrive with headaches and lose the next day.
3. **Forgetting the eILP.** The Bhalukpong gate turns cars back. Apply at least a few days ahead.
4. **Booking a sedan in January.** It saves money until the first icy bend.
5. **No buffer day before a flight home.** The return trip needs the same two days, and a landslide can cost you a third.

## How we'd plan it

For a first visit, we use six nights and keep the driving days light:

1. Guwahati to Dirang (overnight Dirang, about 1,500 m)
2. Dirang to Tawang over the Sela Tunnel (overnight Tawang)
3. Tawang Monastery and town (overnight Tawang)
4. Bum La and Madhuri Lake, permit allowing (overnight Tawang)
5. Tawang to Bomdila or Dirang (overnight)
6. Down to Guwahati, or break at Tezpur or Nameri if your flight is next day
