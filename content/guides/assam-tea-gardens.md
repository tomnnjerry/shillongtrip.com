The first thing you hear at a tea bungalow in Upper Assam is not birdsong. It is the factory whistle at around 6 am, calling pluckers to the sections, followed by the rattle of bicycles on the estate road. By 7 the rows below the verandah are dotted with women in bright headscarves, baskets on their backs, working two leaves and a bud at a time.

That is what a tea garden stay gives you that a hotel in Guwahati cannot. We have been sending guests to Assam's estates for years, and this is how we choose where, when and for how long.

## What a tea bungalow stay actually is

Assam's tea estates were laid out from the 1830s onward, after Robert Bruce was shown the wild Assam tea plant by the Singpho people in 1823. Each estate built bungalows for its managers: big, airy houses, often raised on stilts in the chang style to keep them above flood and damp, with deep verandahs and a lawn facing the tea.

A handful of these are now run as guest stays. You typically get:

- A large room with high ceilings, period furniture and a verandah
- Home-style meals cooked by the bungalow staff, often Anglo-Assamese
- A guided walk through the tea sections with a staff member
- A factory visit and tasting when the estate is plucking
- Quiet evenings with no traffic, sometimes a bonfire in winter

What you do not get is a resort. There are no pools at most bungalows, Wi-Fi can be patchy, and dinner is at a fixed time. For the right traveller, that is the point.

## Where to base yourself

Assam grows tea in two valleys, but for a stay the choice is really between three bases in the Brahmaputra Valley.

| Base | Nearest airport | Pairs well with | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jorhat | Jorhat (Rowriah) | Majuli, Kaziranga, Sivasagar | Central and practical |
| Dibrugarh | Dibrugarh (Mohanbari) | Dibru-Saikhowa, river cruises | Deep tea country |
| Balipara, near Tezpur | Tezpur or Guwahati | Nameri, Kaziranga | Close to the hills |

**[Jorhat](/places/jorhat/)** is where we send most first-timers. It is home to the Tocklai Tea Research Institute, founded in 1911, and sits about 15 km from Nimati Ghat, the ferry point for [Majuli](/places/majuli/). Kaziranga is roughly 90–100 km west, and the Ahom monuments of [Sivasagar](/places/sivasagar/) are about 55–60 km east. Thengal Manor at Jalukonibari, a heritage house built in 1929, is one of the best-known stays in the area.

**[Dibrugarh](/places/dibrugarh/)** calls itself the tea city of India, and the drive in from the airport runs past estate after estate. It suits guests who want tea as the main event, with a boat day on the Brahmaputra or a trip to Dibru-Saikhowa. Our [Dibrugarh and Dibru-Saikhowa](/packages/upper-assam-4n/) trip is built on this base. Several heritage chang bungalows operate around the town; we match one to your dates and budget.

**Balipara, near [Tezpur](/places/tezpur/)**, sits on the north bank of the Brahmaputra with the Arunachal foothills on the horizon. Wild Mahseer, a heritage property of planter-era bungalows on about 22 acres surrounded by tea, is roughly 25 km from Tezpur. From here [Nameri National Park](/places/nameri/) is close and Kaziranga is a comfortable day trip.

## Plucking seasons month by month

Tea is a crop, and the estate you visit changes completely with the calendar. The dates below are typical; weather shifts them by a few weeks either way.

| Period | What is happening | Factory | Our verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mid-Dec to Feb | Pruning, bushes resting | Mostly idle | Quiet, cool, few tea activities |
| Late Mar to Apr | First flush | Starting up | Good, fresh green, pleasant days |
| May to Jun | Second flush, the prized tippy tea | Busy | Great tea, hot and humid |
| Jul to Sep | Rains flush, highest volume | Full production | Wet, very green, leeches on walks |
| Oct to Nov | Autumn flush | Running | Our favourite: dry, clear, active |
| Early Dec | Last plucking | Slowing down | Pleasant, cool mornings |

The **second flush** is what the world thinks of as Assam tea: strong, malty and full of golden tips. If you are a serious tea drinker, a May or June stay lets you taste it fresh from the factory, but be ready for 32–35°C afternoons and high humidity.

**October and November** balance everything. The autumn flush is being plucked, the monsoon has cleared, and Kaziranga reopens in stages for the season. This is when our bungalow calendar fills first.

## What you do on an estate

A typical day at a tea bungalow runs like this:

- **6:30 am:** tea on the verandah as the pluckers head out
- **8:00 am:** breakfast
- **9:00 am:** a walk through the sections with an estate guide, including how shade trees are used and how plucking is done
- **11:00 am:** factory visit, following the leaf through withering, rolling, oxidation and drying
- **12:30 pm:** a tasting, comparing orthodox and CTC teas and different flushes
- **Afternoon:** rest, cycling on estate roads, or a village or bazaar visit
- **Evening:** sundowner on the lawn, dinner at a fixed hour

Estates are working farms and private property. Visits to the tea sections and the factory are arranged by the bungalow with the estate management; walking into a factory uninvited is not allowed. Some factories do not permit photography inside, and food-safety rules may mean hairnets and closed shoes.

About the pluckers: they are working, often paid by weight. Ask before photographing anyone, keep a respectful distance, and do not hand out money for posed photos. If you want to try plucking, the estate guide will arrange a short, supervised go.

## Orthodox or CTC: knowing what you are tasting

Assam makes two broad kinds of black tea, and you will taste both.

- **Orthodox** tea is rolled whole or in large pieces. It gives a nuanced cup that people drink without milk, and it is what specialist shops sell by flush and grade.
- **CTC** (crush, tear, curl) turns the leaf into small granules that brew fast and strong. It is the everyday tea of Indian kitchens and the base of most chai.

At a tasting, ask to compare a first-flush orthodox, a second-flush tippy orthodox and a CTC side by side. The difference surprises nearly everyone.

## Who a tea stay suits, and who it does not

We are honest with guests about this, because a bungalow is a specific kind of holiday.

**It suits you if:**

- You like slow mornings, long walks and early nights
- You drink tea seriously, or want to learn
- You are travelling with older parents who want comfort without stairs or long drives each day
- You want a calm break between Meghalaya's hill roads and Kaziranga's 5 am safari starts
- You are a birder: estates with old shade trees attract a lot of species, and Balipara in particular sits close to good forest

**It may not suit you if:**

- You want nightlife, shopping or restaurants to choose from each evening
- You need strong Wi-Fi for work every day
- You are visiting between mid-December and February mainly to see plucking
- You dislike insects; an estate in the rains is full of life, not all of it welcome

## Choosing between bungalows

Two bungalows 30 km apart can feel completely different. These are the questions we ask on your behalf before recommending one:

1. **Is the estate plucking on your dates?** A bungalow can be open in January while its factory is shut.
2. **How many rooms are there?** Three rooms feel like a private house; ten feel like a small hotel. Both are fine, but know which you are booking.
3. **What is included?** Some rates are room only, some include all meals, and some include the factory visit and tasting. Compare like with like.
4. **How far is it from the road you need?** A bungalow 45 minutes down estate tracks is lovely until you need a 5 am start for Kaziranga.
5. **Does it have backup power?** Most do, but ask, especially for summer stays when fans and air-conditioning matter.
6. **Is it a working estate or a former one?** Some stays sit on land that was once part of a garden, with the working estate next door. Factory visits then depend on the neighbour.
7. **Are children welcome?** Nearly all are family-friendly, but a few with antique furniture and open verandahs ask families to keep a close eye on toddlers.

## A monsoon stay, if you are curious

Most planners tell you to avoid June to September. We say go if you understand the trade.

The estates are at their greenest, the factories run all day, and you will have the bungalow largely to yourself. Rain comes in heavy bursts rather than all day, and mornings are often clear. What you give up: Kaziranga's main ranges are closed, walks can be muddy with leeches in the sections, humidity sits above 80% and roads flood in places. For a pure tea-and-rest trip of 2–3 nights from Jorhat, it can work well. Check current status before you travel.

## How long to stay and what it costs

**Two nights** is the minimum. One full day covers the walk, factory and tasting without a rush. **Three nights** lets you add a day trip to Majuli, Sivasagar or Nameri.

As an indicative 2026 guide, per room per night for two:

- Heritage tea bungalows: roughly INR 6,000–15,000, usually with breakfast or all meals
- Flagship suites at top properties: well above that, at times INR 30,000+
- Simpler estate guest houses and Jorhat town hotels: much less

Prices move with the season, and several bungalows have only three to six rooms. Confirm rates and meal plans in writing.

## Practical notes before you book

- **Getting there:** Jorhat and Dibrugarh both have direct flights from Kolkata and Delhi on most days. From Guwahati, Jorhat is about 300 km by road, 6–7 hours.
- **Mosquitoes:** estates are green and wet. Carry repellent and full-sleeve clothes for evenings.
- **Weather:** October to March days are 20–28°C, with December and January mornings dropping to around 8–10°C. Pack a warm layer.
- **Connectivity:** mobile data works in most bungalows, but do not plan video calls.
- **Alcohol:** available in Assam; bungalows usually serve or allow it.
