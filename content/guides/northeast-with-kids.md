The question parents ask us most is not "will the kids like it" but "will the kids survive the car". Fair enough. Meghalaya's roads are all bends, and a child who is sick on the first drive can colour the whole trip. Get the pacing right, though, and this is one of the best family destinations in India: caves to crawl through, waterfalls you can hear before you see, a village where every child is handed a broom, and rhinos a few hours down the road in Assam.

## When to go with children

The dry months are simply easier with kids. Steps are less slippery, boats run at Dawki, views clear more often and you lose fewer hours to fog.

| Months | With kids | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| October–November | Best | Green hills, falls still flowing, clear days, Kaziranga reopening |
| December–February | Very good | Clear and dry; Shillong nights average about 6 °C in January; pack warm layers |
| March–April | Good | Warmer, some showers; Kaziranga open, hot by April |
| May | Fair | Pre-monsoon storms start; Kaziranga closes for the monsoon |
| June–September | Harder | Heavy rain, fog, slippery trails, boating often suspended |

School holidays matter too. Diwali and the Christmas–New Year break are the busiest family weeks, and Shillong's hotels and roads fill up. Book two to three months ahead for those dates.

## Pace: the rule of three hours

Our standard for family trips is no more than three to four hours in the car on any day, and never two long drives back to back. Here are the drives most families do.

| Drive | Distance (approx.) | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Guwahati Airport to Shillong | 120 km | 3–3.5 hours |
| Shillong to Sohra | 55 km | 1.5–2 hours |
| Shillong to Mawlynnong | 80 km | 2.5–3 hours |
| Mawlynnong to Dawki | 30 km | 45–60 minutes |
| Dawki to Shillong | 82 km | 2.5–3 hours |
| Shillong to Guwahati Airport | 120 km | 3–3.5 hours |
| Guwahati to Kaziranga | 195 km | 4.5–5 hours |

That means two nights in each base, not one. Children cope with a long day if they know tomorrow is a slow one.

**Car sickness tips that actually work:**
- Seat the prone child in the middle row, facing forward, window slightly open.
- Light breakfast, not an empty stomach.
- Ask your paediatrician about a remedy before the trip.
- Stop every 60–90 minutes; drivers here know the tea stops with clean toilets.

## Sights children love in and around Shillong

**[Elephant Falls](/places/elephant-falls/)** is 12 km from the city, with three tiers and a stepped path between them. Small children can see the first tier with a short walk; the lower tier takes more steps. Allow an hour.

**[Umiam Lake](/places/umiam-lake/)** sits on the Guwahati highway 15–17 km before Shillong, so it fits into arrival day. Boating and water sports run on some parts of the lake in fair weather.

**Don Bosco Museum** in Mawlai has seven floors on the cultures of the Northeast, plus a rooftop skywalk. It works well on a rainy afternoon and gives older children context for everything else they will see.

**Ward's Lake** is a short walk from Police Bazar: paddle boats, a bridge and ducks, perfect for the first evening.

**Shillong Peak** is the highest point near the city at about 1,965 metres, inside an Air Force area. Carry ID for every adult. On a clear morning children can see most of the city below.

## Sohra with kids: caves, falls and short walks

Sohra (Cherrapunji) is the part of the trip children tend to remember.

- **[Mawsmai Cave](/places/mawsmai-cave/)** is a lit limestone cave with a one-way path. It takes 20–30 minutes, with a few narrow squeezes that children find far more fun than adults do.
- **Nohkalikai Falls** is viewed from a railed platform a few minutes from the car park. The drop is about 340 metres, so keep toddlers in hand.
- **Seven Sisters Falls** is a roadside viewpoint, best from June to October.
- **Arwah Cave** has fossils in the rock; the approach is a paved walk of about 15–20 minutes.
- **Eco Park** has flat paths and views over the plains of Bangladesh on clear days.

One caution: many viewpoints across Meghalaya, especially Laitlum Canyons, have long unfenced edges and grass that ends in a drop. Keep small children on a hand or in a carrier.

## Root bridges: which one for which age

This is the decision parents most often get wrong, because every photo shows the Double Decker at Nongriat.

| Root bridge | Walk | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| Riwai, near Mawlynnong | 10–15 minutes down steps, same back | All ages, carriers fine |
| Other bridges around Sohra and Pynursla | 20 minutes to 2 hours of steps, by bridge | 8+ with a local guide |
| Double Decker, Nongriat | About 3,000 or more steps each way, 2–3 hours each way | Fit 10+ and teens |

With a keen teenager, the Double Decker is one of the great family days out in India; read our [Double Decker trek guide](/guides/double-decker-root-bridge-trek/) first. With younger children, Riwai gives the same moment of standing on a bridge made from living roots, without the climb.

## Mawlynnong and Dawki

**[Mawlynnong](/places/mawlynnong/)** is a small Khasi village famous for its cleanliness. Bamboo dustbins stand along every path, and children tend to notice that nobody drops anything. The bamboo Sky View platform gives views towards Bangladesh on clear days. Homestays here are basic, so most families visit for the day or stay one night at most.

At **Dawki**, the Umngot river is clear from roughly November to April and the rowboat rides are a hit with children. Boats carry life jackets; insist that everyone wears one. In the monsoon the river runs brown and fast and boating is often suspended. See our [Dawki and Umngot guide](/guides/dawki-umngot-guide/) for timings.

## Adding Kaziranga

A jeep safari in [Kaziranga](/places/kaziranga/) is where many children see their first wild rhino, often within the first half hour. Zones such as Central (Kohora) are 4.5–5 hours from Guwahati. The morning safari slot is cold in winter and the afternoon slot is easier for small children.

- The park normally closes from May to September and reopens in stages from October.
- Carry binoculars; one pair per child avoids arguments.
- Confirm age rules and child fees for the jeep and elephant safaris when you book.

Check current status before you travel. Our [Kaziranga safari guide](/guides/kaziranga-safari-guide/) covers zones and booking.

## Tawang and high altitude with young children

Arunachal's Tawang route is spectacular, but it is not a first family trip. The road climbs to about 4,170 metres at Sela Pass, and Tawang sits near 2,700 metres. Children adjust to altitude less predictably than adults and may not say they feel unwell.

If you are set on Tawang with children:

- Talk to your paediatrician before booking.
- Sleep at Dirang (about 1,500 metres) on the way up.
- Keep the first Tawang day easy and skip Bum La until day two or three.
- Watch for vomiting, unusual sleepiness or refusal to eat; if symptoms appear, descend.

For most families with under-sevens, we suggest Meghalaya and Kaziranga first, and Tawang when the children are older.

## Hotels, food and health

**Hotels.** Shillong has the widest choice of family rooms and interconnecting rooms. In Sohra, pick a hotel within 15 minutes of the main viewpoints so you can pop back for naps. In winter, ask whether rooms have heaters and hot water all day.

**Food.** Shillong has rice and dal, noodles, momos, pizza, bakeries and cafés. Khasi food is mostly pork, chicken and rice, mildly spiced. Carry breakfast basics, snacks and any baby food brands you rely on; small-town shops stock few.

**Health.** Shillong has hospitals including NEIGRIHMS, and Guwahati has large multi-speciality hospitals. Beyond the city, facilities are basic. Pack a fever medicine, ORS, a thermometer, plasters and your child's usual prescriptions.

**Toilets.** Main viewpoints and cafés have paid or free toilets; long village roads do not. Carry tissue and hand sanitiser.

## Keeping children engaged on the road

The Northeast rewards curious children, and a little preparation turns long drives into part of the fun. Things that have worked for families we have hosted:

- **A spotting list.** Give each child a card: a pine forest, a betel-nut tree, a bamboo bridge, a tea garden, a one-horned rhino, a river boat, a church on a hilltop. First to twelve wins the front seat.
- **A rain gauge game in Sohra.** Tell them Sohra and nearby Mawsynram take turns as the wettest places on earth, then ask them to guess how many buckets fell on the hotel roof last night.
- **Market mornings.** Iewduh (Bara Bazar) in Shillong is one of the largest traditional markets in the region. It is crowded and steep in places, so go early, hold hands and stick to the fruit, bamboo and spice lanes for 30–45 minutes.
- **Make something.** Several villages and craft shops sell bamboo and cane work; a small basket or toy is a better souvenir than plastic.
- **A journal.** Children who draw one thing each day remember far more than those who only take phone photos.

We also tell parents to give children a job: holding the map, choosing the lunch stop, counting steps at Elephant Falls. It helps with the bends.

## Booking tips for families

A few things to ask before you pay any deposit, whether you book with us or anyone else:

- **Child policy.** Hotels in Shillong and Sohra usually allow one child without an extra bed; older children or a second child need an extra bed or an interconnecting room. Ask for the exact age cut-offs.
- **Vehicle size.** A family of four with luggage needs an SUV or MPV such as an Innova or Ertiga class vehicle, not a hatchback. Two families usually need a Tempo Traveller or two cars.
- **Child seats.** They are rarely available locally. If your child needs one, bring a lightweight booster.
- **Flight times.** Pick flights that land in Guwahati before 1 pm so you reach Shillong before dark, and leave after 12 pm on the last day.
- **Refund terms.** Weather or a closed road can change a day. Refundable or flexible rooms are worth the small difference.

## Three generations on one trip

Many of our family trips include grandparents, and the Northeast suits them if the plan respects two different paces. Children want to run down the steps at Elephant Falls; a 70-year-old knee does not want to climb back up.

What works for mixed-age groups:

- Choose viewpoints with a short, level approach for the whole group, such as Nohkalikai, Seven Sisters, Umiam and Ward's Lake, and send the energetic half to the longer walks.
- Book ground-floor or lift-access rooms; many hill hotels are built down the slope.
- Keep one car for the group if possible, so nobody is stranded while others trek.
- Build in a free afternoon every second day.
- Avoid overnight stays in basic village homestays unless everyone is comfortable with shared bathrooms.

## How we'd plan it

For families with children under twelve, we build a five-night Meghalaya trip that looks like this:

1. **Day 1:** Guwahati Airport to Shillong, Umiam Lake stop, Ward's Lake in the evening.
2. **Day 2:** Shillong at a slow pace: Elephant Falls, Don Bosco Museum, Shillong Peak if clear.
3. **Day 3:** To Sohra: Mawsmai Cave, Nohkalikai, Eco Park.
4. **Day 4:** Arwah Cave or a short walk, afternoon free at the hotel.
5. **Day 5:** Mawlynnong, Riwai root bridge, Dawki boats (in season), back to Shillong.
6. **Day 6:** Shillong to Guwahati Airport.
