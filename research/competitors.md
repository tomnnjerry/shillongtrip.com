# Competitors and UX patterns — Shillong Trip

Compiled 30 Sep 2026. Target queries: "meghalaya tour package", "shillong tour package", "northeast tour package", "kaziranga tour package", "tawang tour package".

**Method note:** our web-search quota ran out partway through, so we did not measure exact rank positions. The 10 sites below are the ones that kept appearing in results across our NE travel queries this session. Where we fetched a page (30 Sep 2026), we say so. Prices are the advertised "from" prices per person on that date.

---

## 1. The ten sites we are up against

### 1. Thrillophilia — thrillophilia.com/meghalaya-tour-packages (fetched)
- **Title / H1:** "35 Meghalaya Tour Packages, Book @ Upto 40% Off" / "Meghalaya Tour Packages".
- **H2s:** Thrillophilia Exclusives, Best Time to Visit, Places to Visit, Top Things to Do, Shopping.
- **Strengths:**
  - Huge review count (they claim about 15k reviews on this page).
  - Named-package hooks: "FREE Nongriat Trek", "FREE Jeep Safari".
  - Filters by duration, budget and origin city.
  - Sample 4, 7 and 10-day itineraries, plus a cost breakdown.
  - A "People Also Ask" FAQ.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Permanent "up to 40% off" discounting erodes trust.
  - Duplicate cards and dense text.
  - Two competing CTAs ("Request Callback" and "Connect With Expert").
  - Generic inclusions ("comfortable hotels").
  - The /tawang- and /kaziranga-tour-packages URLs redirected to the homepage when we fetched them, which is a gap.

### 2. Holidify — holidify.com (Meghalaya, Tawang and Kaziranga package pages fetched)
- **Model:** a marketplace. "Compare quotes from 3 agents, save up to 30%".
- **Scale:** 127 Meghalaya, 70 Tawang and 52 Kaziranga packages.
- **Strengths:**
  - Budget, mid-range and premium price tiers on one card.
  - Agent ratings.
  - FAQs that cover entry fees and best time.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Near-identical Shillong–Sohra–Dawki itineraries.
  - Agent-quality variance.
  - A factual slip: the Kaziranga page says "closes May–October". The park actually opens in phases from late Sep/Oct.

### 3. SOTC — sotc.in/india-tour-packages/north-east-tour-packages (fetched)
- **H1:** "North east Tour Packages".
- **FAQs:** how to reach, best time (Nov–Apr), cost ("from ₹21,400"), how many days (14–16).
- **Strengths:** a legacy brand, toll-free numbers and modular customisation.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - The "North East" list is padded with Darjeeling and Sikkim.
  - Visible template bugs ("{{loggedInUserFirstName}}", and FAQs showing "No Data Found").
  - Weak visual hierarchy.

### 4. MakeMyTrip Holidays — makemytrip.com/holidays-india/meghalaya-tour-packages.html (timed out; known SERP presence)
- **Strengths:** brand trust, EMI and payment options, bundled flights and dynamic pricing.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Its content is a thin wrapper around its listings.
  - No depth on permits, weather or safari logistics.

### 5. Yatra Holidays — yatra.com/india-tour-packages/meghalaya-tour-packages (timed out; known SERP presence)
- Same OTA pattern as MakeMyTrip: price-led cards and little destination expertise.

### 6. TourMyIndia — tourmyindia.com (Arunachal permit page fetched)
- **Strengths:**
  - Deep state-by-state guide pages that rank for informational queries (for example "permit for foreign tourists").
  - 8–28-night NE itineraries.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - **No prices shown**; everything is "request a quote".
  - Dated layout and dense navigation.
  - Its Meghalaya packages URL redirected to the homepage.

### 7. NortheastToursPackages.in (homepage fetched)
- **Title:** "Northeast India Tour Packages 2026 — From ₹14,499 (137 Plans)".
- **Strengths:**
  - A "Trip planner" tool.
  - Permit guides.
  - Month-by-month and budget guides ("₹1,500–3,000/day").
  - A year-stamped title.
  - The closest thing to our content model.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - No concrete packages with day-by-day itineraries and prices on the hub.
  - Leans on affiliate links (Klook, Aviasales, Hotellook).

### 8. Kaziranga-national-park.com (and similar single-park "official-looking" domains)
- **Strengths:**
  - Exact-match domains.
  - Blog posts on opening dates rank quickly every September.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - These domains look official but aren't, which is a trust risk.
  - Thin on anything outside the park.
  - We avoid mimicking this pattern.

### 9. TravelTriangle / Pickyourtrail (agent-quote marketplaces; TravelTriangle URL returned 404)
- **Strengths:** a lead-form-first flow and multi-agent quotes.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Aggressive callback forms.
  - Generic copy.
  - Unmaintained URLs, which is an opportunity for us.

### 10. Bikat Adventures / Indiahikes-style trek operators (permits blog seen in results)
- **Strengths:**
  - Credible on treks and permits.
  - Clear difficulty grading.
- **Weaknesses:** no general-tourist packages (Kaziranga, family trips).

**Shared keyword and heading patterns:**
- "[Place] Tour Packages" as the H1, with "N Nights / N+1 Days" in card titles.
- "Upto X% Off" in the title tag.
- "Best Time to Visit [Place]", "Places to Visit", "How to Reach", "Things to Do" and "Honeymoon / Family / Group".
- "from ₹X", "2026" in titles, and "ILP / Inner Line Permit".
- Almost nobody targets "Kaziranga safari booking + zones", "Bum La permit", "Meghalaya guest registration" or "rainfall by month" with a real tool. That is our opening.

## 3. Design and UX patterns worth borrowing (for "Monsoon Modern")

1. **Trip-card fact strip (Exodus, Much Better Adventures).**
   - Exodus cards show days, "from" price, trip type, activity tag and star rating with review count on one line.
   - *For us:* a rounded 28 px image-left card with a single sticker row: `5N/6D · Easy · Max 1,965 m · From ₹17,500`.
   - It works because people compare trips by scanning cards, not by opening them.
2. **Activity or difficulty grading with an explained scale (Wilderness Travel, Intrepid "physical rating", G Adventures).**
   - *For us:* 1–5 dots with a hover or tap tooltip, for example "3 = up to 4 h walking a day, 3,000 m+ nights".
   - It works because Tawang altitude and Nongriat's 3,500 steps are real decision factors.
3. **"Book with confidence" trust cards (Exodus: "Deposit Freedom", "Price-Lock Promise").**
   - *For us:* three honest pill cards: "Permits handled", "Flood/landslide re-route plan" and "Local drivers from Shillong and Guwahati".
   - Only publish promises we actually keep.
4. **Day-by-day itinerary with a map and route line (Intrepid, G Adventures).**
   - *For us:* the route stepper. Each day is a node with overnight altitude and drive (km · h), and a sticky mini-map highlights the day in view.
   - It works because road hours are the #1 surprise in the NE.
5. **Month-by-month "when to go" (Jacada, Wilderness Travel).**
   - *For us:* the Rain meter (IMD bars) on every place page, plus a places × months heatmap. Green means best, teal means rainy but open, grey means closed (for example Kaziranga in Jun–Sep).
6. **Multi-step enquiry with specialist framing (Jacada).**
   - Jacada asks when, who, budget and style before any contact field.
   - *For us:* a 3-step pill-button form (month → group type → budget band) that ends in **WhatsApp or email**, with UTM attribution stored.
   - It works because short taps beat a 9-field form on mobile.
7. **Departure and date table with availability (Intrepid, G Adventures, Much Better Adventures).**
   - *For us:* only for fixed festival departures (Hornbill 1–10 Dec, Ziro late Sep). A rounded table with "Confirmed / Filling / Waitlist" tags, updated by hand.
8. **"At a glance" fact card overlapping the hero (Much Better Adventures, Intrepid "trip highlights").**
   - *For us:* an overlapping white card with 6 facts: best months, permit, altitude, nearest airport, ideal stay and from price. It doubles as a clean source for AI answer engines.
9. **What's included / not included as icon lists (G Adventures).**
   - *For us:* two columns with tick and cross stickers. Always list safari jeep, ILP fee, Bum La permit, boat at Dawki and guide at Nongriat explicitly, because these are where Indian OTAs stay vague.
10. **Editorial guide hub with seasonal hooks (Jacada content hub, Exodus blog).**
    - *For us:* a bento grid of guides with category chips (Permits, Seasons, Wildlife, Treks) and "updated 30 Sep 2026" stamps.

## 4. How we beat them

1. **We publish real from-prices** for every package, split into Standard, Premium and Luxury tiers. TourMyIndia and most agent-quote marketplaces hide theirs.
2. **We never run fake permanent discounts.** No "up to 40% off" badges, no countdown timers, no fake "12 people viewing".
3. **We itemise the things OTAs blur:** Kaziranga jeep and entry (₹100 Indian / ₹650 foreign), ILP fee, Bum La permit, and whether the Dawki boat is included.
4. **We build the permit checker (state × passport).** It covers the Dec 2024 PAP reimposition for Manipur, Nagaland and Mizoram and the new 2026 Arunachal ILP guidelines. Nobody else shows both.
5. **We build a Kaziranga safari planner** with zones, jeep and elephant slots, the phased Oct opening and camera fees. It turns the yearly "opening date" search into our page.
6. **We add a Rain meter on every place page** using IMD 1991–2020 normals. Sohra gets 2.6 m in July alone; no competitor shows this visually.
7. **We add road-time honesty on every day:** km, hours and overnight altitude, with Tawang packages at 6N minimum. Rushed 4N Tawang itineraries are a common complaint category.
8. **We cover Meghalaya's 2025–26 rules** that competitors ignore: mandatory guest registration, no self-drive rentals and the planned entry registration. We write about them calmly and factually.
9. **We follow a strict AEO structure:** a 40–60 word quick answer, a fact card, exactly 9 FAQs, JSON-LD (TouristTrip, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList) and `llms.txt`. SOTC's FAQs literally show "No Data Found".
10. **We make WhatsApp-first shortlists.** Save packages to a shortlist and send it in one tap, with attribution. We don't ambush people with callback pop-ups.
11. **Our design is unmistakable:** Monsoon Modern with a grotesk display face, 28 px cards, bento grids and a light footer with a green CTA panel. The category is saturated with cluttered OTA templates and stock-photo heroes.
12. **We use credited Commons photos of the actual place,** refresh every guide with an `updated` date each season, and write "check current status before you travel" on every volatile fact. That trust compounds over time, while competitors copy last year's blog.
